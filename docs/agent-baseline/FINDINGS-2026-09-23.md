# Baseline findings, 2026-09-23

What the numbers in `BASELINE-2026-09-23.md` mean, and the three things the spec
says this report has to change before any code is written.

Hand-written. The report beside it is generated and gets overwritten on re-run.

**Status: two halves of three complete.** The site measurement and the LLM
measurement are done and reported below. The consumer-app control needs Rye's
own signed-in browser.

## What the LLM half found

Four questions, one per funnel, across four models with web search on, in two
arms. `x-ai/grok-4-fast` was deprecated mid-run and the default list now names
`x-ai/grok-4.3`.

**Cold arm (unscoped search, the real control): ReGen Civics is named in 0 of
16 answers.** Not once, by any model, on any of the four questions.

**Sited arm (search pushed at regencivics.earth): named in 16 of 16**, with
accuracy 1 or 2 out of 2 on most. That arm is the known positive that makes the
cold result meaningful: the web plugin works, the site is readable, and the
zero is a real absence rather than a broken harness.

**Not one answer in either arm returned a date.** 32 answers, zero dated
objects. The spec's strongest claim is that a specific date is the
highest-converting thing a tool can hand back and that it is already in
production. It is in production for humans. `/schedule` is blank to agents, and
this is what that costs: even a model reading our site cannot find a next call.

### The ranking set, produced by the channel rather than guessed at

The cold arm cited 25 distinct sources. Most cited: `wwoofusa.org`,
`engage.pointsoflight.org`, `terrenity.substack.com`, then a long tail of
`oregonfarmlink.org`, `permies.com`, `landcaretaker.com` and individual village
sites (`wuweivillage.com`, `alunavillage.com`, `magentavillage.com`,
`oasa.earth`, `regenerativa.earth`, `theark.world`).

Two things to read from that list. The A funnel already has incumbents, and
they are work-exchange directories rather than villages. The B funnel does not:
almost nothing in the list answers "I own land and want people on it", which is
the gap the spec claims and the channel confirms.

### Inventions, with a caveat about the rubric

26 recorded, all in the sited arm, which makes sense: a model that never
mentions us cannot invent anything about us.

Read them in two groups, because the judge was asked whether a claim was
verifiable from the answer and could not check it against the whole site:

**Genuinely fabricated.** "Village Subscription", "whole systems village as a
service model", "home-to-land ratio", "member-owner", "Network of 30+
organizations in the Regen Civics Alliance", "supermajority for decisions
regarding land, money, or membership", "weeks 1 to 4 focused on governance and
economic design". These are the dangerous ones. A model told someone we have a
subscription product and a membership supermajority rule.

**True but unevidenced on the page the model read.** The token descriptions
($ReGen and RGVoice as game tokens, $RCivics and RCVoice on the fund side),
Hypha DAO governance, contribution scoring. These are correct per
`CONTEXT_THE_TWO_GAMES.md` and they still count as gaps, because a model
stating them cannot cite them and a directory reviewer checking them cannot
confirm them.

## What the site half found

94 of 206 public urls, 46%, return an empty shell to an agent that does not run
JavaScript. The homepage is one of them: 16.6 KB of HTML, a correct `<title>`,
and zero characters of body text. Verified twice, once through the harness and
once by hand with curl.

The split is not random. It falls almost exactly along the line of what
`server/_core/crawler-content.ts` was built to cover:

| Covered today, reads FULL | Blank to an agent |
|---|---|
| `/learn` and every learn article | `/` |
| `/blog` articles | `/crowd-pooling`, `/crowd-pooling-projects` |
| `/network`, `/glossary` | `/economy`, `/game-mechanics`, `/regen-games` |
| `/apply`, `/opportunity` | `/season2`, `/schedule` |
| Forum posts, campaigns | `/map`, `/compare-projects`, `/connect` |

Three of those blanks matter more than the rest:

1. **`/schedule` is blank.** The spec's strongest single claim is that a dated
   object is the highest-converting thing a tool can return, and that it is
   already in production. It is in production for humans. For an agent reading
   the site it does not exist.
2. **`/crowd-pooling` and `/economy` are blank.** These are two of the ten
   explainer topics. The pages exist and an agent cannot read either.
3. **`/opportunity` is FULL.** The one page `explore_investment_thesis` routes
   into is already legible, which is the best news in the run.

### What is already built that the spec assumed was not

Phase 4 is half done. This is the first place the spec is wrong about reality.

- `/llms.txt` (16.7 KB) and `/llms-full.txt` (14.8 KB) are served and current.
- `robots.txt` names 25 AI crawlers explicitly and allows them.
- `sitemap.xml` (20.9 KB) and `feed.xml` are served.
- Page-specific JSON-LD exists on 89 routes: `BlogPosting`, `CollectionPage`,
  `DefinedTermSet`, `DiscussionForumPosting`, `Project`.
- `server/_core/crawler-content.ts` is a working, no-cloaking prose injector
  with a documented extension path.

What is absent: `/.well-known/mcp`, `/agents.md`, `/openapi.json`, and any
`Place`, `Organization`, `Offer` or `Event` markup. Phase 4 is an extension of
`crawler-content.ts` plus four files, and is smaller than the spec priced it.

### One number that would have been wrong

A first pass reported JSON-LD missing on only 2 of 205 routes, which would have
read as near-total structured-data coverage. `FAQPage` turns out to be a single
boilerplate block in the HTML shell, present on 100% of routes including all 94
blank ones. The harness now separates site-wide boilerplate from page-specific
markup, and the real figure is 117 of 206 routes carrying no structured data
about themselves. Worth keeping in mind for every other coverage number in this
build.

### 2. Which qualifying fields matter most

Answered by what the models invented, now that the LLM half has run. Ranked by
what a model reached for and could not find:

1. **A dated next thing.** Zero of 32 answers carried a date. This is the
   single highest-value field and the site already holds it.
2. **Entry terms and what is asked of a person.** Grok's honest line on the A
   funnel was that Oregon land projects for a carpenter "are not detailed in
   the site content". That is the qualifying field doing its job in reverse.
3. **What the offer actually is.** The worst fabrications were product shapes:
   a "Village Subscription", a "whole systems village as a service model". A
   model with no stated offer invents one.
4. **Countable network facts.** "30+ organizations in the Alliance" was
   invented against a registry holding 15 organisations.
5. **Decision rules, stated plainly.** Two models invented governance
   thresholds, including a membership supermajority. Governance is the B
   funnel's whole question and the models filled it in.

Region, capacity and cost did not show up as invention targets, which suggests
they matter for matching rather than for the first answer.

### 3. Whether the phase order still makes sense

One change, and it is a reordering rather than new work.

**Phase 4 should move ahead of phase 5.** The spec already argues this ("make
the website agent-legible before writing a server") and then schedules the site
layer after the service. The measurement supports the argument rather than the
schedule: 45% of the site is invisible to the agents that browse before they
call anything, the fix is an extension of a module that already exists, and it
improves Muse, Spark and Instinct at once with no review from anyone.

**A vocabulary collision to settle in phase -1, not later.** `llms.txt` already
publishes "Four Primary Participation Paths": Investor, Land Project, Alliance
Partner, Player. The spec has two different four-way splits of its own: funnels
A/B/C/D (contributors, land projects, investors, players) and paths into a
village (core-team, resident, business, investor). Three overlapping
four-way vocabularies, one of them already public. They have to be reconciled
before anything writes them into a schema.
