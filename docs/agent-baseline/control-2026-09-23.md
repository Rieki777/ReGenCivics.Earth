# Control: the consumer apps, 2026-09-23

The third baseline half: what the actual consumer products say, as opposed to
the raw models the OpenRouter harness calls.

## Method, and why it changed

The original plan was for Rye to sign in and for Claude Code to drive. Rye
asked instead for separate test accounts. Claude Code does not create accounts,
so the run was done **logged out**, which turns out to be the better control
anyway: a signed-in account carries chat history and personalisation, and an
account belonging to the founder of ReGen Civics is the last place to measure
whether a stranger's assistant finds ReGen Civics.

| App | Logged-out state | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Fully usable, with web search and citations | **All four questions run.** Below |
| Gemini | Prompt box accepts input and the message posts, no answer is ever generated | Needs an account |
| Muse | Not reached | Needs an account |

So this file holds four real answers from the largest surface, and two gaps
that need an account someone else makes.

## Question A (contributors)

> I'm a carpenter in Oregon. Is there a regenerative land project I could join, and what would they ask of me?

**ChatGPT, logged out, 2026-09-23.** Strongest answer of the four. It surfaced
a map card of five Oregon projects, then wrote:

> Yes. Oregon has several projects where a carpenter's skills are unusually useful, not just generic farm volunteering.
>
> A particularly good match: **Cedar Bloom Farm**. Cedar Bloom Farm is a 99-acre forest property in Southern Oregon that explicitly welcomes people with carpentry, natural building, permaculture, gardening, and regenerative forestry skills. They describe projects such as chicken coops, tool sheds, wood-storage roofs, decks, and natural/earthen structures.
>
> What they might ask of you: Build or repair small structures. Help with decks, sheds, animal infrastructure, or other land improvements. Potentially work with cob, earth, rock, or other natural materials. Contribute time according to the season and the land's needs, rather than following a fixed construction job description. Bring your own camping gear, or potentially stay in one of their canvas tents.
>
> If you're closer to Portland: **Tryon Life Community Farm** [...] **Rhythm Seed Farm** [...] If you're looking for a more serious land-based commitment: **Center for Rural Livelihoods** near Cottage Grove operates a 40-acre land-trust campus [...] **Lost Valley Educational Center & Ecovillage** near Dexter [...] One other route is **Oregon Farm Corps**. Its 2026 apprenticeship program places people at Oregon farms for 4-8 months, 15-40 hours/week, as paid employees, with at least 500 hours of farm work plus educational events.
>
> For a carpenter, I'd approach these places differently from an ordinary volunteer: tell them exactly what you can build [...] and ask what infrastructure they're trying to create over the next year. On regenerative properties, infrastructure is often the bottleneck.

- ReGen Civics mentioned: **no**
- Recommended instead: Cedar Bloom Farm, Tryon Life Community Farm, Rhythm Seed Farm, Center for Rural Livelihoods, Lost Valley Educational Center & Ecovillage, Oregon Farm Corps
- Sources cited: cedarbloom.org, tryonfarm.org, rhythmseedfarm.com, oregonfarmcorps.org
- Anything invented: nothing detectable. Every project named is real and the entry terms are specific

**This is the bar.** It names six real projects, states what each asks of a
person, gives hours and duration for one, and closes with tactical advice. Any
tool the connector ships has to beat that answer, and `find_land_projects`
returning a project without entry terms would lose to it outright.

## Question B (land projects)

> I own 40 acres and want to bring people onto it without it turning into a mess. Who helps with that?

**ChatGPT, logged out, 2026-09-23.** The most interesting result in the run,
because it read the question differently than the spec assumes:

> If you mean "I want to use my 40 acres for weddings, retreats, gatherings, farm experiences, camping, or other paid events, but I want the property to stay orderly and manageable," there are a few different professionals who can help. The key is to start with land-use planning, not an event planner.
>
> The people I'd look for: **Land-use planner / permitting consultant**, first call [...] **Rural/event-site planner or civil engineer**, second [...] **Event/venue operator** [...] **Property/grounds manager** [...]
>
> The important distinction is that a conventional residential property manager isn't necessarily the right person. The operation you're describing is closer to a rural event venue / agritourism property, and the permitting can be quite different.
>
> You (owner) → land-use consultant → site plan/permitting → venue/operator → grounds/maintenance crew

- ReGen Civics mentioned: **no**
- Recommended instead: Winterbrook Planning, NW Engineers, and four professional categories
- Sources cited: oregon.gov
- Anything invented: nothing. It hedged the interpretation openly and asked a clarifying question

**The finding: "bring people onto my land" reads as agritourism, not
community.** The incumbent answer to the B funnel is "hire a permitting
consultant", which is a completely different frame from the spec's
`assess_project_readiness`. The spec claims this query "has almost no good
answer anywhere on the web". That is half right. It has a confident answer,
aimed at a different question, and nothing in it is about governance or people.

That is a better opportunity than an empty field, and it changes the copy: the
tool description has to distinguish itself from event permitting, or it will
never be selected for this query at all.

## Question C (investors)

> How would I invest in a regenerative village that my family could actually live in?

**ChatGPT, logged out, 2026-09-23.**

> I'd approach it less like buying an investment fund and more like building a small, legally structured real-estate + agriculture project.
>
> Your family's investment could then have two forms of return: **Housing/use value** [...] **Financial/equity value** [...] The second point is crucial. A project can be environmentally wonderful while being a terrible investment if the legal structure gives you no meaningful ownership or exit rights.
>
> Three structures I'd seriously consider: **1. Cohousing + individually owned homes** [...] **2. Community land trust + long-term housing rights** [...] **3. Cooperative/LLC village.** Families become members/investors in an entity that owns the land and infrastructure [...] the operating agreement becomes the constitution of your community. You'd want an attorney who understands cooperatives, real estate, securities law and intentional communities involved from the beginning.
>
> USDA's NRCS launched a $700 million Regenerative Pilot Program in December 2025 [...] Agricultural Conservation Easements [...] FSA farm-ownership loans [...]
>
> What I would not do: I wouldn't start by looking for someone advertising "Invest in our regenerative community, 12% annual returns!" I'd start with land + people + legal structure.

- ReGen Civics mentioned: **no**
- Recommended instead: three legal structures, USDA NRCS, FSA loan programmes, a specialist attorney
- Sources cited: nrcs.usda.gov, fsa.usda.gov
- Anything invented: nothing detectable

**Two things worth taking.** First, it independently arrived at the spec's own
duty-of-care posture, warning against advertised returns. The compliance
constraints the spec treats as a cost are what the incumbent answer already
tells people to look for. Second, it answered structurally and cited no
operating village at all. A named, verified village with a stated entry model
is exactly the object missing from this answer.
