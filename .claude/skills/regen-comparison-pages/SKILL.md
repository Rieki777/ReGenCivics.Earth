# ReGen Civics Comparison Pages

## What this skill does

Write comparison pages that pass three tests at once:

1. The other project's team would read it and say "yes, that's a fair
   description of what we do."
2. A new visitor reads it and walks away with a sharper sense of what
   ReGen Civics is for, even if they end up choosing the alternative.
3. The page surfaces real differentiators, not manufactured ones.

This is the opposite of "vs" pages that dunk on competitors. The
regenerative space is not a competitive market. We collaborate with most
of the projects we'd compare against, and our goal is healthier movement,
not market share.

## The mental model

Three categories of "alongside":

| Category                | Examples                                  | Framing                                              |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Movement allies**     | SEEDS, Hypha, Kernel, Regen Network       | "We work with them. Here's the division of labor."   |
| **Adjacent funds**      | Regen Network grants, Refi DAO, Gitcoin   | "Different theory of change. Here's when each fits." |
| **Different paradigm**  | Traditional VC, ESG funds                 | "Different game entirely. Here's the trade."         |

The framing dictates the tone. Movement allies get warm cooperation.
Adjacent funds get respectful contrast. Different paradigm gets
clear-eyed critique without contempt.

## The page structure

### 1. Lede (1-2 paragraphs)

Name the alternative, link to it, and state plainly when each one fits.
Lead with respect. Don't open with "unlike X" or "where X falls short."

Pattern:

> **ReGen Civics and [Project] are both working toward [shared north star].
> [Project] focuses on [their specific lane]. We focus on [our specific
> lane]. Both can be true at once, and many of the same people participate
> in both.**
>
> Here's how to know which one fits where you are right now.

### 2. The "use them when" / "use us when" table

Two columns. Specific scenarios. Real, not strawmen.

| Use [Project] when...                                  | Use ReGen Civics when...                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| You want a [specific tool / network / outcome]         | You want a [specific tool / network / outcome]                 |
| You're already in [their network / context]           | You're entering through [our entry point]                      |
| You need [their strength]                              | You need [our strength]                                         |

Three to five rows. Each row is a real, plausible scenario. If a row reads
as "use them when you want a worse outcome, use us when you want a better
one," delete it; that's a strawman.

### 3. How we work together

This section is the giveaway that the page isn't adversarial. Spell out
the actual integrations, partnerships, or shared participants.

For ReGen Civics specifically, the real integrations are:

- **SEEDS:** historical contributors get SEEDS-claim conversions to
  $ReGen on the new ledger. The SEEDS-vision spec is core context for how
  we think about money flows.
- **Hypha:** we run formalization through Hypha DAO contracts on Base.
  Forum decisions become DHO proposals. Tokens move on-chain via Hypha.
- **Regen Network:** ecological credit verification standards inform our
  HEIST framework (Health, Equity, Impact, Sovereignty, Trust).
- **Gitcoin:** quadratic funding patterns inform some of our pool
  mechanics; many community members participate in both.
- **Kernel:** cohort-style learning containers inspire our incubator
  cadence.

If a comparison target has no real integration, say so plainly. "We
don't currently integrate with [Project]; here's where we might in the
future."

### 4. Where we differ on substance

This is the part of the page that earns its keep. Pick 2-4 substantive
differences. Each gets a paragraph that names the difference, names the
trade-off, and is willing to admit when their choice fits a use case
ours doesn't.

Examples of substantive differences for ReGen Civics:

- **Fund structure.** We chose a venture-capital-style fund intentionally,
  for legibility to mainstream capital allocators. SEEDS chose a
  community-owned cooperative model. Each fits different fundraising
  contexts.
- **Game layer.** ReGen Civics ships a literal in-real-life game with
  quests, seasons, and contribution scores. Most adjacent projects are
  pure financial / governance infrastructure. The game is doing real
  work in onboarding people who'd never otherwise touch a regen fund.
- **Two-token bridge.** Our four-token model (RGVoice, $ReGen, RCVoice,
  $RCivics) splits governance from economic value, and Game from Fund.
  Most peer projects use a single-token or two-token model. The
  complexity buys us legibility on both sides of the chasm. The cost is
  more cognitive load on participants.
- **Land-project incubator.** Our investment model channels capital to
  bioregional land projects through a season-based incubator, rather
  than open grant rounds or token treasury allocations.

Each difference should pass the test: "would the comparison target's
team read this and feel it represents them fairly?"

### 5. CTA section

Two CTAs. Prominent. Side by side or stacked.

- **For people aligned with ReGen Civics:** apply, contribute, join the
  game.
- **For people aligned with the alternative:** link to the alternative
  with a real recommendation. "If [their thing] is what you're looking
  for, [Project] is the place to go. Tell them we sent you."

The willingness to send people away is what makes the page trustworthy.

## Voice rules (binding)

These ride on top of the project-wide Writing Rules. Specific to
comparison pages:

- **No strawmen.** If the description of the alternative makes them sound
  obviously worse, rewrite. The test: would their CEO sign off on this
  description if you sent it to them?
- **No "they're great, but..."** This is contrast framing in disguise.
  Just say what each is. The reader can draw their own conclusion.
- **No "the only solution that..."** Almost certainly false; if true,
  prove it with a source.
- **No "we're not like other funds."** Either show a real difference or
  cut the line.
- **Specific over comparative.** "ReGen Civics seasons run March to
  September with 13 stewardship roles" is better than "we have a more
  structured cohort experience than most."
- **Em-dashes banned.** Zero. Project-wide rule.

## Anti-patterns (will get the page rejected)

- A "feature comparison checklist" with green checkmarks under our column
  and red Xs under theirs
- "Unlike [Project], we actually..."
- "[Project] is for X. ReGen Civics is for serious X."
- Quoting the alternative's marketing copy out of context
- Claiming integrations that don't exist
- "We respect [Project], but..."
- Lists of "limitations" of the alternative

## Patterns that work

- Two real people both quoted: one who chose the alternative, one who
  chose ReGen Civics, both happy. ("I'm also active in [other thing] and
  here's why both make sense for me.")
- A diagram showing the actual division of labor (Hypha = on-chain
  governance contracts, ReGen Civics = the game layer that originates
  proposals, SEEDS = the prior community whose ledger we honor).
- An honest "considered both, here's why I went with..." testimonial
- A closing line that names the shared purpose: "However you participate
  in the Regenerative Renaissance, the work matters. Pick what fits."

## When NOT to write a comparison page

- The "competitor" is a fundraising round Rye is hoping to participate in
  himself. (Conflict of interest.)
- The space is genuinely hostile (predatory token projects, scammy
  "regen-washing"). In that case write a values post about what regen
  isn't, not a comparison page.
- We don't actually compare. If 80% of visitors aren't choosing between
  ReGen Civics and the named project, the page is academic.
