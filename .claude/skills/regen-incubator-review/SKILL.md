# ReGen Civics Incubator Application Review

## What this skill does

Read a land project's application (LOI, written submission, video,
pitch deck, or a free-form description) and produce a structured
review that lets Rye and the season cohort make a decision quickly.

The bottleneck for the incubator is not deal flow. The bottleneck is
review bandwidth as applications scale. This skill gives every
application the same lens.

## The six-dimension rubric

Score each dimension 0-5. Add written rationale for each score. Don't
average to a single number; the dimensions are not commensurable.

### 1. Bioregional fit (0-5)

Does the project sit in a real, recognizable bioregion? Is the project
oriented to bioregional regeneration rather than an extraction-style
"green" play?

- **5:** Named bioregion, named watershed, real-world location with
  ecological context. Explicit bioregional governance frame.
- **3:** Recognizable region. Some ecological context. Bioregional
  framing implied, not centered.
- **1:** Generic "we're regenerating land" with no place-rooted detail.
- **0:** Project is not actually rooted in land at all.

### 2. Ecological practice (0-5)

What is the project's actual practice? Soil, water, biodiversity, food,
forest, fiber. Is it grounded in observable methods rather than
movement-language?

- **5:** Specific practices with named lineages (Holistic Management,
  Permaculture Designer Certificate, Mark Shepard / Joel Salatin
  influence, Fukuoka, Indigenous land-back partnership, etc.).
  Quantified plot, acreage, species, or yield where available.
- **3:** Real practice in the language of the practice. Some
  numbers. Plausible scale.
- **1:** Vague "regenerative agriculture" without specifics. No
  numbers.
- **0:** Marketing language only. No practice substance.

### 3. Governance readiness (0-5)

Can this project participate in season-cohort governance, on-chain
proposal flow, and bioregional coordination? Or is it solo-founder-driven
with no governance scaffolding?

- **5:** Multi-stakeholder governance already in place. Clear decision
  protocol. Comfortable with on-chain governance via Hypha. Open to
  shared bioregional learning.
- **3:** Solo or small-team but governance-curious. Willing to
  participate.
- **1:** Solo-founder ego. Reluctant to share decisions.
- **0:** Active red flag (control-seeking, opaque, hostile to
  governance review).

### 4. Financial structure (0-5)

Is the project structured in a way that can receive and deploy capital
from a regen fund? Is there a clear financial plan?

- **5:** Existing legal entity (LLC, cooperative, foundation, etc.).
  Articulated capital ask. Clear use of funds. Audited or self-audited
  books.
- **3:** Legal entity exists or is in formation. Capital ask is in the
  right ballpark. Use of funds is plausible.
- **1:** No entity. Vague ask. Unclear use of funds.
- **0:** Asks for personal-bank-account funding. Or asks for funds without
  any plan for them.

### 5. Team coherence (0-5)

Is the team real? Have they worked together? Are they regen-natives
or movement-curious?

- **5:** Multi-person team. Track record together. Demonstrated
  integration of regen practice. Diverse backgrounds (ecological,
  financial, social).
- **3:** Solo founder with strong individual track record + advisors.
- **1:** New team. Unknown track record. Learning together.
- **0:** Single individual with no demonstrated capacity. Or team
  conflict signaling in the application.

### 6. Community alignment (0-5)

Does the project belong in the ReGen Civics movement specifically?
Will they participate in the game, the seasons, the bioregional
coordination?

- **5:** Already participating (forum member, completed Welcome Aboard
  Quests, attended Open Access Sessions). Knows the language. Aligned
  with bioregional regeneration values.
- **3:** Aligned, new to ReGen Civics, willing to engage. Played in
  adjacent movements (SEEDS, Hypha, Regen Network, Kernel).
- **1:** Brand-new. May not understand what they're applying to.
- **0:** Not actually a regen project (greenwash, extractive with
  green branding, fossil-adjacent, exploitative practices).

**Date:** [Today]

## Snapshot

[2-3 sentences: what the project is, where it is, what they're asking for.]

## Rubric scores

| Dimension              | Score | One-line rationale                                  |
| ---------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Bioregional fit        | X / 5 | [...]                                               |
| Ecological practice    | X / 5 | [...]                                               |
| Governance readiness   | X / 5 | [...]                                               |
| Financial structure    | X / 5 | [...]                                               |
| Team coherence         | X / 5 | [...]                                               |
| Community alignment    | X / 5 | [...]                                               |

## What's strong

- [Specific thing that was clear and compelling]
- ...

## What's unclear or concerning

- [Specific thing that needs follow-up]
- ...

## When to recommend each verdict

- **Advance:** Total scores ≥ 18/30, all dimensions ≥ 2, no zeros, no
  red flags. Or strong scores in 4 dimensions with willingness to
  develop the other 2 during the season.
- **Hold for next round:** Strong scores but not ready this season
  (timing, team gap, capital readiness). Encourage them to apply again.
- **Decline:** Scores are too low to be coachable in a season. Or
  fundamental misfit (not actually regen, governance hostile, etc.).
- **Refer elsewhere:** Real project, not a fit for our specific
  incubator. Common alternatives: SEEDS Switchboard, Kernel cohorts,
  Refi DAO grants, regional bioregional networks, traditional
  conservation grants.

## Voice rules for the review (binding)

- **Direct, not euphemistic.** "Score is low because the application
  doesn't name the bioregion or any specific practice" beats "the
  application could be more developed in some areas."
- **No "exciting opportunity / promising team / inspiring vision."**
  AI-tells. Empty. Banned.
- **Honest about decline.** If the answer is no, the review says no
  with a real reason. Land stewards don't need to be sugarcoated; they
  need the actual call.
- **Refer with respect.** When recommending elsewhere, name the
  alternative and why it fits better. Don't dump them.
- **Em-dashes banned. Project rule.**

## What NOT to do

- Don't write the review in marketing language. This is internal cohort
  decision support, not a public post.
- Don't auto-decline based on weak writing. Many of the strongest land
  stewards are not strong writers; their work is in the soil. Lean on
  evidence of practice over polish of prose.
- Don't average the rubric scores into one number. The dimensions are
  not commensurable. A 5/5 ecological practice with a 0/5 financial
  structure is different from a 3/5 in everything.
- Don't decline without offering follow-up questions. If the answer
  could change with new information, ask first.

## Bias awareness

- **Geography bias:** Don't undervalue Global South or non-English-first
  applications. Translate (or ask Rye to translate) before scoring.
- **Class bias:** Land stewards from working-class / generationally-poor
  backgrounds may not have legal entities or audited books yet. Score the
  intent + capacity to develop, not just the present state.
- **Indigeneity:** Indigenous-led land projects with traditional
  governance count as 5/5 governance readiness even if there's no LLC
  or DAO. Their governance has run for centuries. Don't apply Western
  frames.
