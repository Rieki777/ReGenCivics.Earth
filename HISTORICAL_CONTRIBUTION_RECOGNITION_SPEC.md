# Historical Contribution Recognition System

**Date:** 2026-04-10
**Status:** Design spec. Ready for community input.
**Token:** $ReGen (1 $ReGen = $0.01 USD)
**Scope:** Broad regenerative movement (pre-ReGen Civics contributions included)
**Scale:** 100-500 contributors in the first round
**Vesting:** Locked until Go Live, then vesting over time
**Location:** `/game` page on gov.regencivics.earth
**Claim window:** Open now through September equinox 2026
**Proposal Parties begin:** June Solstice 2026
**Linking:** Blog post, forum post (Air > Onboarding Games > Historical Contribution Accounting), and main site all link to the claim flow on gov.regencivics.earth/game

---

## What this is

The regenerative movement has hundreds of people and organizations who have been building for years. Permaculture teachers, land stewards, tool makers, community organizers, open-source developers, network weavers, researchers, and many others. Most of them have never been properly recognized or compensated for what they've built and what they're bringing to this network.

This system creates a simple, fair, and transparent process for recognizing those historical contributions with $ReGen tokens. The goal: honor what's been done, surface what exists so the movement can use it, and align incentives so everyone has a reason to work together going forward.

We're building one network. This is how we say "we see what you did, and it counts here."

And like everything in the Infinite Game, this process is designed to be evolved by the people who use it. Every contributor who goes through the claim flow is invited to help improve it. The tiers, the rubric, the questions, the Proposal Party format: all governable. All co-created. The first version is a seed. The community grows it into whatever it needs to be.

---

## Design principles

**Simple over precise.** Tiers exist to be egalitarian while keeping the process easy. We're placing people and organizations into clear bands, not asking them to itemize every hour they've ever worked. Nobody should need an accountant to participate.

**Easy and seamless, always.** Every proposed improvement to this system has to pass one test: does it add burden to people's time? If yes, find a way to get the same result without the burden. The regenerative movement is full of people who are already stretched thin. This process should feel like a welcome, not a grant application.

**Self-assessment guided by criteria.** Contributors know their own work best. They choose their tier, but they do it against a clear rubric that makes the decision obvious in most cases. Edge cases get reviewed (admin in early seasons, community in later ones).

**Transparent and challengeable.** Every tier placement is visible to the community. Anyone can raise a question. This builds trust and prevents gaming.

**Forward-looking incentive.** Tokens are minted on recognition but locked until the economy goes live. Every token is backed by real contributions. Everyone benefits from building a healthy economic system together. The recognition is immediate; the value is collective.

**The game evolves through playing it.** This process is itself a game inside the Infinite Game. Every player who goes through it is invited to see what could be better and propose changes. The claim flow, the tiers, the rubric, the Proposal Party format: all of it is governable. Improvement proposals go through the standard governance pipeline: forum (Air > Onboarding Games > Historical Contribution Accounting) -> Gov App (gov.regencivics.earth) -> Hypha for ratification. The community co-creates this system in real time using the governance tools we've built.

## How it works: the Claim Flow

The whole experience starts with one button: **"Claim Your Contributions."**

When a player clicks it, they enter a guided conversation. Friendly questions, one at a time, that feel more like a welcome than a form. The answers do five things at once: onboard the contributor, determine their tier, surface what they bring to the network, route them to the right part of the ecosystem, and prepare them for a Proposal Party where their claim becomes official.

No spreadsheets. No dollar-value calculations. Just a conversation that ends with clarity.

### The question sequence

The flow is a series of screens, one question per screen, with warm contextual text. The tone is welcoming and grounded. Each answer narrows the routing and builds the tier suggestion behind the scenes.

**Screen 1: Welcome**

> We're one network, built by hundreds of people and organizations who've been doing regenerative work for years. Some for decades.
>
> This is how we recognize what you've done and what you're bringing.
>
> It takes about 10 minutes. At the end, you'll know where you fit and how your work connects to the movement.

[Let's go]

**Screen 2: Who are you?**

> Are you claiming as an individual or on behalf of an organization?

[Individual] [Organization]

This forks the flow. Individuals get the 5-tier rubric. Organizations get the 3-tier rubric. The questions that follow are tuned to each.

**Screen 3: What's your name?**

> Individual: Your name and handle (or the name you go by in the movement).
> Organization: Your organization's name and a one-line description of what you do.

Text input. Simple.

**Screen 4: What forms of capital are you bringing?**

> The regenerative movement runs on more than money. We recognize 8 forms of capital. Pick every form that describes what you're contributing. Most people bring several.

Checkboxes (multi-select), each with a one-line description:

- **Intellectual**: Research, writing, curricula, frameworks, methodologies, publications, open-source code
- **Experiential**: Years of practice, skills earned through doing, apprenticeships, hands-on knowledge you carry in your body and your work
- **Social**: Relationships, networks, trust, community connections, the ability to bring people together and hold space
- **Material** : Tools, technology, platforms, physical infrastructure, land access, equipment, built things others can use
- **Financial** : Fund design, grant programs, economic models, currency experiments, investment infrastructure
- **Living (Ecological)** : Land stewardship, regenerative agriculture, food production, conservation, seed saving, ecosystem restoration
- **Cultural** : Art, storytelling, music, media, ceremony, language, traditions that carry meaning through the movement
- **Spiritual** : Healing practices, wellness, contemplative traditions, sacred relationship with land and community

Each selection opens a short follow-up: "Give a one-line example." (e.g., "Built a soil carbon measurement tool" or "15 years facilitating community circles.") These examples feed the tier suggestion and help with routing later: ecological contributors get a LocalScale nudge, material/intellectual contributors get a Tools Library prompt, social contributors get governance invitations.

**Screen 5: How long have you been at it?**

> How long have you been actively contributing to regenerative work?

[Under 1 year] [1-3 years] [3-5 years] [5-10 years] [10+ years]

Maps to the "track record" dimension. For organizations, the question is slightly different: "How long has your organization been operating?"

**Screen 6: Who have you reached?**

> How many people has your work directly served, taught, connected, or supported?

Individual: [Under 50] [50-200] [200-1,000] [1,000+] [Hard to count, it's generational]
Organization: [Under 500] [500-5,000] [5,000+]

Maps to "reach and influence" / "network reach."

**Screen 7: What have you built?**

> What tangible things have you created that others can use? Pick all that apply.

Checkboxes:
- A tool, software, or platform
- A curriculum, course, or training program
- A methodology or framework
- Templates, guides, or documentation
- A physical space, farm, or land project
- A network, community, or organizing structure
- Publications, books, or research
- Art, media, or cultural work
- Financial infrastructure (fund, grant program, currency)
- I've contributed time and energy but haven't built a named thing (that's real too)

For each selected item, a follow-up asks: "Give it a name and a one-sentence description." If someone built five things, they can name the top two or three and skip the rest. These feed directly into the Tools Library and the contributor's profile.

**Screen 8: Tell us more**

> In your own words, describe what you've done and what you're bringing to this network. A few sentences is fine. A few paragraphs is fine too.

Textarea with no minimum word count. This is the heart of the claim. It becomes the contributor's description on the public Contributors page and the core of their Proposal Party presentation. Some people will write two sentences. Some will write a page. Both are valid.

**Screen 9: Evidence**

> If you have links to share (website, project page, publication, photos), drop them here. Totally optional.

Multi-input field (add as many links as you want). Helpful for the review team but not required. Many contributors do their best work offline or in spaces that don't have URLs. That's fine.

**Screen 10: What's alive for you now?**

> One sentence: what are you bringing to this network going forward?

Text input. This appears on their contributor profile and signals to the community what kind of collaboration this person is open to.

**Screen 11: Your suggested tier**

Based on the answers, the system suggests a tier. The screen shows:

> Based on what you've shared, we'd suggest you're at the **[Tier Name]** level.
>
> [Tier description from the rubric]
>
> This means your historical contribution claim would be for **[X] $ReGen tokens** (valued at $[Y] USD at $0.01/token), locked until the economy goes live, then vesting over 18 months.
>
> Does this feel right?

[Yes, that's right] [I think I should be higher (explain why)] [I think I should be lower]

If they select "higher," a text field appears for their reasoning. The review team will see both the system suggestion and the contributor's argument.

**If the system detects above-tier scale:** When an individual's answers suggest contributions that exceed Old Growth level ($150K+), or an organization's answers exceed Mycelium level ($1M+), Screen 11 shows a different message:

> Based on what you've shared, your contributions are at a scale that goes beyond our standard tiers. That's a good thing. It means what you've built is significant enough that it deserves a different kind of relationship with the network.
>
> Two paths forward:

This means a token swap (your existing assets, IP, or infrastructure for $ReGen at a negotiated rate) and a seat at the fund level. This is how the network's biggest contributors become its economic partners.
>
> [Claim as an organization] [Learn about the Fund pathway] [Continue with individual tiers anyway]

This is the routing screen. Based on their forms of capital (Screen 4) and tangible outputs (Screen 7), the system shows personalized next steps:

> Here's how your contributions connect to the network:

Conditional sections (only the relevant ones appear):

### After the flow: what happens behind the scenes

**1. Onboarding.** The contributor now has a profile, a pending claim, and personalized links to the parts of the ecosystem that match their work. They're in the network.

**2. Tools Library.** Every tool, curriculum, methodology, or resource they named in Screen 7 gets queued for the Tools Library with the contributor listed as the creator. A community editor can clean up descriptions before they go live, or they publish as-is.

**3. LocalScale routing.** Contributors who produce food, goods, or services get a warm handoff to Local Scale where they can list offerings and accept $ReGen. This creates a real economic loop: recognition -> tokens -> trade -> more recognition.

**4. Tier review.** In Seasons 1-2, admin reviews each claim directly. Starting Season 3, three randomly assigned Stewards/Sages take over. Reviewers see the system's suggested tier, the contributor's self-assessment, all the answers, and any evidence links. They confirm, suggest an adjustment (with reasoning), or flag for a conversation.

**5. Forum thread.** If the contributor submitted an improvement idea (Screen 13), it's posted to Air > Onboarding Games > Historical Contribution Accounting. The idea is tagged with their name and tier, and the community can respond. Ideas that gain traction can become governance proposals.

**6. Proposal Party.** The contributor is invited to the next Proposal Party. This is where people share their proposals, meet each other, and the community formally welcomes new players into the Game. After the party, the claim is published on the Contributors page and the locked $ReGen allocation appears on their passport.

### The Proposal Party

This is the beating heart of the process. Equal parts introduction party and proposal sharing event. Starting at the June Solstice, these are regular gatherings where people share who they are, what they've built, what role they play, and what they're bringing to the Game across any of the 8 forms of capital.

For many contributors, their contribution IS their life's work. This party is the moment where that life's work gets seen and honored by a room full of people who are building the same future. It's a big introduction. It's "here's what I've spent my years on, and here's how it serves this network."

**Format:**
- 75-90 minutes, three acts
- **Act 1: Introductions and Proposals** (45-60 min). Each person gets 3 minutes to share: who they are, what their role has been in the movement, what they've built, and what forms of capital they're bringing to the Game. Then 2 minutes of community Q&A and connection. ("Wait, you built a soil testing methodology? Our land project needs that." This happens constantly.)
- **Act 2: Welcome and Confirmation** (10 min). The community does a collective confirmation (simple hand-raise or governance vote for any contested tiers). Presenters' names and tier badges go live on the Contributors page in real-time. This is the formal welcome into the network.
- **Act 3: Game Evolution** (15 min). The facilitator reads out improvement suggestions submitted during the claim flow (from Screen 13). The community discusses the top 2-3 ideas live. Anyone can champion an idea and take it to a governance proposal through the standard pipeline (forum -> Gov App -> Hypha). This is how the process evolves each round.

**Why a party, not just a form submission:**
- For many people, their contribution is their life's work. Sharing it deserves more than a text box.
- It's an introduction party. People meet each other and discover shared work they didn't know about.
- It surfaces collaboration opportunities in real time.
- It holds the process accountable in public.
- The Game Evolution segment means the process gets better every time it runs.
- It's fun. Governance should be joyful sometimes.

Contributors who can't attend live can submit a 3-minute video instead. Their presentation plays at the party and the community confirms in the same way. They can also submit improvement ideas asynchronously via the forum thread.

### Review process (between claim and Proposal Party)

**Seasons 1-2: Admin review.** The admin team reviews all claims directly. This keeps the process fast and consistent while the system and culture around it mature. Each claim gets a quick check (5 minutes, not an audit) and one of three outcomes:

- **Confirmed** at the suggested tier
- **Adjustment** suggested up or down with a short reason
- **Flagged** if the claim needs a conversation

If an adjustment is suggested, the contributor sees the reasoning and can accept it or request a conversation before the Proposal Party. Flagged claims get resolved through a direct conversation with admin. The goal: every claim is resolved before the next Proposal Party.

**Season 3+: Community review.** Once the process has run for two seasons, review shifts to the community. Three reviewers (randomly assigned from the Steward/Sage pool) do a quick check on each claim. 2 of 3 confirms = approved. Flagged claims go to a 5-person review circle (async, forum-based) for a final determination within 7 days.

90% of claims should be confirmed on first pass. The rubric is designed to make most placements obvious. The review process is intentionally light. We trust people to know their own work.

### After the Proposal Party: recognition goes live

Approved claims are published on the site's Contributors page with:
- Contributor name and story (from Screen 8)
- **Founder badge** (all first-round contributors)
- Tier badge (Seed through Old Growth, or Roots through Mycelium)
- $ReGen allocation (locked, with vesting schedule visible)
- Links to tools/resources they've contributed (now live in the Tools Library)
- "What I'm bringing forward" statement (from Screen 10)
- Link to their Proposal Party presentation (video recording)

### The forum thread: where the game evolves

Every Proposal Party generates (or continues) a forum thread in **Air > Onboarding Games > Historical Contribution Accounting.** This is the living home for the meta-game. It contains:

- All improvement suggestions submitted during the claim flow (Screen 13)
- Discussion from the Game Evolution segment of each Proposal Party
- Community proposals for changing the process (new tier definitions, rubric adjustments, new contribution categories, flow improvements)
- A running changelog: "Round 2 changes: added a 'community health' contribution type based on @fern's suggestion from Round 1"

The Air section of the forum is where the Infinite Game's design conversations live. Onboarding Games is the category for all the games that help people enter and root themselves in the network. Historical Contribution Accounting is the first thread. Over time, other onboarding games will get their own threads.

Any idea in this thread can be picked up and turned into a formal governance proposal (Draft -> Discussion -> Polling -> Staged -> Hypha). The process for improving the process uses the same governance pipeline as everything else. No special track. If the community wants to change how tier placements work, or add a new tier, or restructure the rubric, they propose it and the community decides.

### Vesting

Tokens are allocated at the Proposal Party but locked. They vest after the Go Live moment (when the $ReGen economy has enough activity, liquidity, and governance participation to be considered healthy). Vesting schedule after Go Live: 25% at Go Live, then 25% every 6 months over the following 18 months. Full access at Go Live + 18 months.

Everyone has a stake in making the economy work. The tokens aren't worth anything until we collectively make them worth something.

## Organization tiers

Three tiers, assessed across four dimensions. Revenue is one signal but not the primary one. Screen 4 captures *what forms of capital* the organization brings. These dimensions measure *how deep* that capital runs:

**Network reach:** How many people does this organization serve, connect, or mobilize? (social capital depth)
**Asset depth:** What tools, knowledge, content, land access, or infrastructure has this organization built? (material + intellectual capital depth)
**Movement track record:** How long has this organization been active in regenerative work? What has it shipped? (experiential capital depth)
**Current vitality:** Is the organization actively operating and growing, or is it dormant with valuable archives?

### Tier 1: Roots ($20,000 USD / 2,000,000 $ReGen)

Organizations that have built something real but are early in their journey or operating at a small scale.

Typical profile:
- A local permaculture education center with a small but dedicated following
- A new regenerative land project that has secured land and started building
- An open-source tool project with a working prototype and a small user base
- A community currency experiment serving one neighborhood
- A research group that has published findings but hasn't scaled distribution

Criteria (meet most of these):
- Active for 1+ years in regenerative work
- Serving or connecting fewer than 500 people
- Has produced at least one tangible asset (tool, curriculum, publication, land improvement, methodology)
- Operating budget under $100K/year or pre-revenue
- Bringing at least one tool/resource to the network's Tools Library

### Tier 2: Canopy ($200,000 USD / 20,000,000 $ReGen)

Organizations with established programs, growing communities, and proven track records.

Typical profile:
- A regional regenerative agriculture network connecting dozens of farms
- A well-known permaculture school with years of graduates
- A regenerative finance platform with active users and transaction volume
- A bioregional organizing body that coordinates across multiple communities
- A media/publishing organization with significant reach in the regen space

Criteria (meet most of these):
- Active for 3+ years in regenerative work
- Serving or connecting 500-5,000 people
- Multiple tangible assets (tools, programs, networks, land projects, publications)
- Operating budget $100K-$1M/year or generating revenue
- Recognized by peers as a reference point in their domain
- Bringing multiple tools/resources to the network

### Tier 3: Mycelium ($1,000,000 USD / 100,000,000 $ReGen)

Organizations that are infrastructure for the movement. Networks of networks. The ones that, if they disappeared, hundreds of other projects would feel the gap.

Typical profile:
- A global regenerative network connecting thousands of projects
- A major regenerative finance fund or platform
- An established movement organization with decades of track record
- A technology platform used by hundreds of regenerative organizations
- A land trust or commons steward managing significant ecological assets

Criteria (meet most of these):
- Active for 5+ years in regenerative work (or absorbed the legacy of organizations that were)
- Serving or connecting 5,000+ people
- Has created infrastructure that other organizations depend on
- Operating budget over $1M/year or managing significant assets
- Widely recognized across the regenerative movement
- Bringing foundational tools/infrastructure to the network

### Beyond Mycelium: the Fund pathway

Some contributions are bigger than the tier system can hold. An organization that has built infrastructure worth $5M, $10M, or more to the regenerative movement doesn't fit neatly into a $1M recognition. Trying to make it fit cheapens both the contribution and the system.

For these contributors, the path is a **token swap and direct partnership with the ReGen Civics Fund.** This means:

- A negotiated $ReGen allocation based on the specific assets, infrastructure, or value being brought in
- The contributor swaps existing assets (IP, platforms, land access, financial infrastructure, network reach) for $ReGen at a rate agreed between the contributor and the Fund's governance
- The contributor becomes a Fund-level partner with governance weight that reflects their contribution
- The swap goes through a governance proposal (forum -> Gov App -> Hypha) so the community ratifies the terms

This keeps the tier system clean and egalitarian for the 95% of contributors it's designed for, while giving the network an honest pathway for the biggest contributions. The tier system is the front door. The Fund pathway is for the people whose contributions are the building itself.

Contributors who enter through the Fund pathway still present at a Proposal Party. They still get a profile on the Contributors page. The community still formally welcomes them. The difference is in the economics: their allocation is custom, not tier-based.

## Individual tiers

Five tiers for individuals. (Not seven. Five is enough to capture the meaningful distinctions without creating agonizing "am I a 4 or a 5?" decisions. Each tier is different enough from the next that self-assessment is straightforward.)

Screen 4 captures *what forms of capital* you bring. These four dimensions measure *how deep* that capital runs:

**Depth of practice:** How long and how deeply have you practiced regenerative work? (experiential capital depth)
**Reach and influence:** How many people have you taught, mentored, organized, or served? (social capital depth)
**Tangible outputs:** What have you built, written, taught, or created that others can use? (intellectual + material capital depth)
**Movement connectivity:** How connected are you across the regenerative ecosystem?

### Tier 1: Seed ($750 USD / 75,000 $ReGen)

You've started your regenerative journey. You've taken courses, volunteered, participated in community projects, or begun integrating regenerative practices into your life and work.

Typical profile:
- Completed a PDC or equivalent training
- Volunteered with a regenerative project for 6+ months
- Active participant in regenerative community spaces
- Beginning to build your own practice or project

### Tier 2: Sprout ($3,500 USD / 350,000 $ReGen)

You're actively contributing. You've built something, taught others, or organized your community around regenerative practice.

Typical profile:
- Running a home-scale or community garden with regenerative methods
- Teaching workshops or courses in your area of expertise
- Active organizer or facilitator in regenerative community spaces
- Created content (writing, video, curriculum) that others use
- 1-3 years of active contribution

### Tier 3: Sapling ($15,000 USD / 1,500,000 $ReGen)

You're a recognized practitioner or contributor. People in your bioregion or domain know your work.

Typical profile:
- Managing a regenerative land project or significant community initiative
- Published author, researcher, or educator in regenerative fields
- Designed tools, methodologies, or systems used by others
- Mentored multiple people in regenerative practice
- 3-5 years of consistent, visible contribution

### Tier 4: Grove ($50,000 USD / 5,000,000 $ReGen)

You're a leader in your domain. Your work has influenced how others practice and organize.

Typical profile:
- Founded or co-founded an organization that serves the movement
- Significant publication or research impact
- Trained hundreds of practitioners
- Built infrastructure (physical, digital, social) that others depend on
- Connects multiple communities or networks
- 5-10 years of high-impact contribution

### Tier 5: Old Growth ($150,000 USD / 15,000,000 $ReGen)

You're a root system of the movement. Your contributions span years or decades and have shaped how regenerative work is understood and practiced.

Typical profile:
- Decades of pioneering work in regenerative practice, organizing, or economics
- Foundational contributions to regenerative theory, design, or governance
- Built or sustained organizations that define the movement
- Mentored a generation of practitioners
- Your name comes up when people talk about the history of this work

## The rubric: making self-assessment easy

The claim form includes a guided self-assessment tool. For each dimension, the contributor answers a simple question and selects from concrete options. The tool then suggests a tier. The contributor can accept or override the suggestion (with an explanation if they go higher).

**For organizations:**

| Dimension | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network reach | Under 500 people | 500-5,000 people | 5,000+ people |
| Asset depth | 1 tangible asset | Multiple assets | Infrastructure others depend on |
| Track record | 1-3 years active | 3-5 years active | 5+ years active |
| Current vitality | Operating, early stage | Growing, established | Mature, movement-defining |

Average score maps to tier: mostly 1s = Roots ($20K). Mostly 2s = Canopy ($200K). Mostly 3s = Mycelium ($1M). Mixed scores default to the lower tier (conservative, community can adjust up).

**For individuals:**

| Dimension | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 | Score 4 | Score 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth of practice | Trained, practicing | Active contributor | Recognized practitioner | Domain leader | Movement pioneer |
| Reach | Local (under 50) | Community (50-200) | Regional (200-1000) | National/network (1000+) | Global/generational |
| Tangible outputs | Participated | Created some things | Created tools others use | Built systems or orgs | Foundational contributions |
| Connectivity | One community | Multiple communities | Regional network | Multi-network | Movement-wide |

Average score maps to tier: 1 = Seed ($750). 2 = Sprout ($3.5K). 3 = Sapling ($15K). 4 = Grove ($50K). 5 = Old Growth ($150K).

## The Founder Designation

Every contributor recognized in the first round earns the **Founder** designation. This is separate from tier. An Old Growth contributor and a Seed contributor are both Founders. The tier reflects the scale of their historical contributions. The Founder badge reflects when they showed up.

Future rounds of historical recognition (if we hold them) will not carry this designation. The Founder badge is specific to the first-round contributors who seized this moment: the particular combination of challenges we faced together at the start, the leap of faith required to claim contributions before the economy was live, and the act of helping build the network before it was built.

Being a Founder means your name will always be in the first cohort. The Contributors page will permanently distinguish first-round contributors with the Founder badge. When people join the network in Season 3 or Season 10 and look back at who was here at the beginning, they'll see your name.

**What the Founder badge includes:**
- A permanent "Founder" designation on the contributor's passport and profile
- A distinct visual badge on the public Contributors page (first-round contributors always visible at the top, or in a dedicated Founders section)
- Recognition in the origin story of ReGen Civics: every Founder is part of the record of how this network began
- First-round minting rates: the founding-round tier amounts are locked as a historical baseline. Future rounds may have different amounts. Founders get the founding rate.
- Governance priority during Go Live: Founder badge holders have a recognized voice in the community vote to "Go Live" on the $ReGen economy

**The Founder invitation is open now, through the September equinox.** After that, the founding round closes. Future contributors are welcome, valued, and recognized. They're just not Founders.

## Tools Library integration

Every claim form asks: "What tools, templates, methodologies, curricula, or resources are you bringing to this network?"

These get catalogued in the Tools Library on the site (the existing tools section). Each tool entry includes:
- Tool name and description
- Creator/contributor attribution (linked to their contributor profile)
- Category (mapped from the 8 Forms of Capital: intellectual, material, ecological, cultural, financial, etc.)
- Access link or download
- Usage notes from the contributor
- Community ratings and usage reports (added over time)

This is one of the most valuable outputs of the whole process. The movement has built hundreds of tools that other projects don't know about. Surfacing them creates immediate practical value for everyone in the network.

## Vesting mechanics

### The Lock

All historical contribution $ReGen allocations are locked from the moment of recognition. They show on the contributor's profile with a "Locked" badge and a clear explanation: "These tokens become active when the $ReGen economy goes live."

The locked tokens are visible in the contributor's passport (Sprint 5 of the Gov app) and on the public Contributors page. Visibility matters. The recognition is real even before the tokens are liquid.

### Go Live criteria

The economy "goes live" when a set of community-governed criteria are met. These are tracked on the Economy Dashboard (Sprint 4 of the Gov app) via the Readiness Gauges:

- **Minimum active players:** 200+ players with Co-Creator tier or above
- **Minimum governance activity:** At least 10 proposals have been ratified through the governance pipeline
- **Minimum token distribution:** $ReGen has been distributed to at least 100 unique holders through quests, seasonal harvests, and historical recognition
- **Community vote:** A formal proposal to "Go Live" passes through the staged seasonal governance process and is ratified on Hypha

The community decides when the economy is ready. The gauges show progress toward that moment.

### Vesting schedule (post Go Live)

- **Go Live:** 25% of locked tokens unlock
- **Go Live + 6 months:** another 25% unlocks
- **Go Live + 12 months:** another 25% unlocks
- **Go Live + 18 months:** final 25% unlocks

Contributors can participate in governance with their full allocation from the moment of recognition (locked tokens still carry governance weight). They just can't transfer or trade them until they vest.

### Continued engagement bonus

Contributors who remain active in the network (participating in governance, contributing to seasonal work, mentoring, attending gatherings) earn a 10% bonus on their remaining locked tokens at each vesting milestone. This rewards people who stick around and keep building, without punishing those who step back.

### Demonstrated value bonus

Historical contributions can earn additional multipliers after the fact. If a tool you contributed to the Tools Library ends up being widely adopted, or a methodology you built becomes standard practice across land projects, or a network you brought in generates significant activity, that original contribution can earn a bonus.

The specific mechanics for how "demonstrated value" is measured and rewarded are a game design challenge the community will co-create through the governance pipeline (forum -> Gov App -> Hypha). This is one of the first games we'll design together. Some starting possibilities for the community to consider: usage-based bonuses for Tools Library entries, referral bonuses when a contributed network brings in new players, or seasonal "most impactful contribution" awards voted on by the community.

The principle: recognition doesn't have to be a one-time event. If your contribution keeps giving, your recognition can keep growing.

## Token economics: mint on recognition

There is no fixed supply of $ReGen. Tokens are minted into existence when contributions are recognized. Every token in circulation represents real value that someone brought to the network: a tool, a methodology, a decade of teaching, a land project, a network of relationships.

This is the core economic principle: **every $ReGen token is backed by actual work, actual assets, actual contributions.** The currency is an accounting of value pooled into our economy. Our soul's work, made legible and tradable.

### How minting works

When a contribution claim is ratified at a Proposal Party, the corresponding $ReGen tokens are minted on Base and allocated to the contributor's wallet (locked, per the vesting schedule). The minting transaction is on-chain and transparent. Anyone can verify what was minted, for whom, and why.

The mint happens through a governance-controlled smart contract. Only ratified claims trigger minting. The contract enforces the tier amounts. No one can mint outside this process.

### Why this matters

A fixed-supply token says "there's a pie and we're dividing it." A mint-on-recognition token says "the pie grows every time someone brings real value to the network." The first model creates scarcity competition. The second creates abundance alignment.

The question changes from "how much of the supply do we allocate?" to "is this contribution real, and does it bring value the movement can use?" If yes, the tokens are created. The supply reflects reality.

### Supply tracking

Even without a fixed cap, we track total minted $ReGen carefully. The Economy Dashboard (Sprint 4 of the Gov app) shows:

- Total $ReGen minted (all time)
- $ReGen minted this season (broken down by: historical claims, seasonal harvests, quest rewards, governance participation)
- Minting rate trend (is the economy growing at a healthy pace?)
- Value backing ratio: total contributions catalogued in the Tools Library and Contributors page vs total tokens minted. This is the transparency metric that lets anyone ask "is this currency backed by real things?"

### Projected minting from first round

For planning purposes, here's what the first round of historical claims would mint:

**Conservative estimate (200 contributors, middle-heavy distribution):**
- 20 organizations: 5 Roots (10M), 10 Canopy (200M), 5 Mycelium (500M) = 710M $ReGen ($7.1M USD)
- 180 individuals: 40 Seed (3M), 60 Sprout (21M), 50 Sapling (75M), 20 Grove (100M), 10 Old Growth (150M) = 349M $ReGen ($3.49M USD)
- Total new minting: ~1.06B $ReGen (~$10.6M USD)

**Larger estimate (400 contributors):**

- Total new minting: ~2.12B $ReGen (~$21.2M USD)

**Plus Fund pathway contributors** (not included above): 2-5 organizations entering through the token swap pathway could add another $5-20M USD equivalent depending on the scale of what they bring. These are negotiated individually through governance.

Each token minted is backed by a named contribution with evidence. The Contributors page and Tools Library serve as the public ledger of what backs the currency.

## What we learned from other systems

This design draws from patterns that worked and avoids patterns that failed:

**From Optimism's RetroPGF:** The retrospective lens (recognizing what's already been done, not predicting future value) and the use of human reviewers supplemented by clear criteria. We adapted their badgeholder model into our three-reviewer system but kept it lighter weight.

**From Coordinape's GIVE circles:** The peer recognition principle. Our community review step is a simplified version. We avoided Coordinape's monthly cadence (too frequent for historical recognition) and their known issue of reciprocity gaming (our random reviewer assignment prevents this).

**From SEEDS' contribution scoring:** The multi-dimensional assessment (community, economic, biosphere). We adapted this into our four dimensions per category. We avoided SEEDS' multiplicative scoring (which penalized organizations weak in any single dimension) in favor of averaging.

**From Commons Stack's Trusted Seed:** The "skin in the game" principle. Our vesting schedule serves the same function, tying recognition to continued engagement without requiring upfront payment.

**From the Token Engineering Commons' Praise system:** The separation of objective evidence and subjective assessment. Our rubric handles the objective dimension; community review handles the subjective.

**What we deliberately avoided:**
- Asking contributors to self-price in dollars (Commons Stack problem: creates wealth-dependent participation)
- Pure peer voting on amounts (Coordinape problem: popularity contest at scale)
- Fully algorithmic scoring (SourceCred problem: misses qualitative and offline work)
- One-time grants with no engagement incentive (RetroPGF problem: cliff risk)

## 10 ways to make it better

**1. Contribution Attestations from peers.** After someone submits their claim, the system automatically asks 3 people they've named as collaborators to confirm the work happened. "Did @cedar really co-design that soil testing methodology with you?" This replaces part of the reviewer burden with people who actually know the work. It's lighter, faster, and more honest than strangers reviewing evidence links.

**2. Living contributions, not just historical ones.** The claim flow asks "what are you bringing forward?" but there's no mechanism to turn that into ongoing recognition. Add a "Seasonal Contribution Update" where recognized contributors can report new work each season and earn additional $ReGen minting. The first claim is the door. Seasonal updates keep the relationship alive. This turns a one-time recognition into a living economic relationship.

**3. Contribution stacking across claims.** If someone claims as an individual AND their organization claims separately, the overlap gets flagged. But the better version: let the system understand that @cedar's individual Sapling claim + Resilient Roots Collective's Canopy claim are connected. Show them as a linked cluster on the Contributors page. This reveals the real topology of the movement, not just a flat list.

**4. A "Vouch" button on every contributor profile.** After someone is recognized, any player in the network can vouch for them. "I've used their soil testing tool. It's real." Vouches accumulate over time and create a secondary trust signal. If someone has 40 vouches from across 8 bioregions, that tells a different story than 2 vouches from close friends. This is soft Coordinape without the gaming problem, because it's not tied to token amounts.

**5. Capital Portfolio radar chart on the Economy Dashboard.** Screen 4 now uses the 8 Forms of Capital (intellectual, experiential, social, material, financial, living/ecological, cultural, spiritual). The next step: aggregate those selections across all recognized contributors and display a radar chart on the Economy Dashboard. "Our network's capital portfolio." It tells us where we're strong and where we have gaps. If 80% of contributors bring intellectual capital and 5% bring financial capital, that's useful signal for recruitment and outreach.

**6. A Contribution Matching system.** When @cedar claims a soil testing methodology and @birch claims a regenerative farm that needs soil testing, the system surfaces the match. "Someone in your bioregion just contributed a tool that's relevant to your work." This turns the claim flow into a network weaver. Every contribution surfaces a connection that might not have been visible before.

**7. Proposal Party recordings as content.** Every 3-minute presentation at a Proposal Party gets recorded and becomes a piece of movement media. A short video library of 200 people and organizations telling their stories. This is incredibly powerful content for outreach, fundraising, and movement storytelling. The presentations should be filmed well (or at least recorded cleanly) and published on the site alongside each contributor's profile.

**8. Graduated minting tiers that evolve.** The tier amounts ($750 through $150K for individuals, $20K through $1M for organizations) are fixed in the first round. But as the economy matures, the community could vote to adjust them. A "Minting Policy" governance proposal could change tier amounts for future rounds. Early contributors got the founding rate. Later contributors might get more or less depending on how the economy has grown. This keeps the system responsive to reality.

**9. A "Contribution Quest" for new players.** Before someone can submit a historical claim, they complete a short quest that teaches them how the system works. Visit the Contributors page, read two existing claims, vouch for one contributor they know, read the tier rubric. This primes them to make a better claim and introduces them to the community's existing contributions. It also prevents drive-by claims from people who haven't engaged at all.

**10. A Value Backing Dashboard.** A public page that shows, in plain language, what backs every $ReGen token. "1.06B $ReGen minted (~$10.6M USD), backed by: 14 tools in the Tools Library, 8 land projects, 42 curricula and training programs, 6 governance methodologies, 23 networks reaching 47,000 people." This is the movement's balance sheet, expressed in contributions instead of dollars. It makes the mint-on-recognition model tangible and gives outsiders (investors, partners, skeptics) a clear answer to "what's this token actually worth?"

## Decisions made

1. **Who reviews claims?** Admin handles all reviews for the first 2 seasons. Community review (Steward/Sage pool) begins in Season 3 once the process and culture around it have matured.
2. **Is there a cap on historical contributions?** No cap at this time. The mint-on-recognition model means supply grows with real contributions. If the community decides a cap is needed later, that's a governance proposal.
3. **Can an individual and their organization both claim?** Yes, but the claims should cover different contributions. Focus your individual claim on what you personally did, and your org claim on what the organization built. Don't double-count the same work.
4. **Can historical contributions earn bonuses after the fact?** Yes. Beyond the continued engagement bonus (10% at vesting milestones), contributions can earn additional multipliers through the Game. For example: if a tool you contributed to the Tools Library turns out to be widely used and valuable, that contribution could earn a bonus. The specific mechanics for "demonstrated value" bonuses are a game design challenge the community will co-create. This is one of the games we'll design together through the governance pipeline.
5. **First claim window:** Open from launch until the September equinox (start of Season 2). This gives contributors roughly one full season to submit their claims, attend a Proposal Party, and get recognized before the next season begins.
