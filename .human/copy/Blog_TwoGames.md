# Blog Post: Mastering Two Games at Once

**Title:** We're Playing Two Games Simultaneously — Here's Why That's the Strategy

**Subtitle:** How ReGen Civics bridges the Dominant Game and the regenerative future without compromising either

**Author:** Rieki Cordon
**Date:** March 2026
**Tags:** strategy, governance, economics, regenerative finance, game design

---

There's a question we get asked a lot, in different forms.

*Why do you have a venture capital fund if you're building a regenerative alternative to venture capital?*

*Why does your governance token have two versions?*

*Why does ReGen Civics feel like it's speaking two different languages at once?*

The answer is the same in every case: because we're playing two games simultaneously. And we think that's exactly right.

---

## The Chasm

Something is shifting in how humans organize themselves and their economies. You can feel it in the growing number of regenerative land projects, decentralized governance experiments, new economic frameworks, and communities choosing to live differently. These aren't fringe movements anymore. They're a growing edge.

But between where most of the world's resources, institutions, and legal structures currently live — what we call the **Dominant Game** — and the regenerative future being built at that edge, there is a chasm. A real one.

On one side: the existing system. Venture capital, traditional legal structures, institutional investors, growth-at-all-costs metrics, centralized power. This is where most capital is. This is where most people's mental models of how the world works are still anchored. It's showing its cracks, but it's still the dominant reality.

On the other side: a growing diversity of **new Games**. Regenerative land projects, decentralized autonomous organizations, community-governed land trusts, contribution-based economies, governance systems that give power to participants rather than shareholders. These are not one unified alternative — they're many experiments, many communities, many approaches. They're alive and growing. They're often underfunded, underconnected, and underestimated.

And between them: a chasm that most people don't know how to cross.

ReGen Civics exists to build the bridge.

---

## Why You Need Two Anchors

A bridge needs to be anchored on both sides to hold. That's not a metaphor — it's engineering.

If you build your bridge only from the new side, it's beautiful and you can see clearly what it connects to. But it doesn't reach the old shore. Capital can't flow across it. Institutions don't trust it. People stuck in the Dominant Game can't find the on-ramp.

If you build your bridge only from the old side, you speak the language of institutions, you can move capital, you have credibility with mainstream investors. But you've reproduced the old Game's logic in a new wrapper. Impact investing with better branding. The bridge leads nowhere genuinely new.

You need both anchors.

That's why ReGen Civics has two distinct spaces — and why they're designed to remain distinct, rather than collapse into each other.

---

## The Fund: Our Anchor in the Old Game

The **ReGen Civics Fund** is our anchor in the Dominant Game.

We chose one of the most powerful coordination structures the old system has produced: a venture capital fund. Not because we love venture capital. Because it's legible. Institutional investors, legal systems, and mainstream capital markets understand it. And that legibility lets us move real, serious capital into regenerative land projects that actually need it.

The Fund is governed by **RCVoice tokens**. Contributions to the Fund are tracked and rewarded in **$RCivics**. These tokens give governance weight over how the Fund's profits and distributions are allocated — a transparent, accountable system for stewarding capital at scale.

The Fund operates by rules the Dominant Game recognizes. On-chain transparency via Hypha DAO. Land-backed security (real soil, real water, real food-forests — not derivatives). A 90% Unity Model that prevents small groups from capturing the fund's direction. Impact measurement via the HEIST framework, which counts soil health and biodiversity alongside financial returns.

It plays enough of the old Game's rules to be effective within it. And it bends those rules toward regenerative ends.

---

## The Game: Our Anchor in the New Games

The **ReGen Civics Game** is our anchor in what's being built on the other side of the chasm.

The Game is not tied to the Dominant Game's economic logic. It's where we experiment, play, and build the systems that don't fully exist yet. It's governed by **RGVoice tokens**, earned through genuine participation: completing quests, contributing to the community, engaging with the ecosystem. Not purchased. Not inherited. Earned.

Contributions to the Game are recognized in **$ReGen** — and what $ReGen becomes is something we're co-creating together as a community. Current possibilities include: an in-game currency for day-to-day interactions, a tracker for how donations and investments flow into the Game, a rewards token where holding more earns you more rights and powers, a foundation for governance mechanisms we haven't designed yet. The specific form matters less than the principle: $ReGen is the currency of the future we're building, not an extension of the system we're moving beyond.

The Game is where culture forms. Where governance wisdom develops. Where people get real experience of what it feels like to make decisions collectively, to earn voice through contribution, to be part of something that isn't organized around profit maximization. It's where the Regenerative Renaissance is actually lived, not just theorized.

---

## How They Work Together

These two spaces are separate by design. They need to be. The Fund must operate within old Game rules to be effective there. The Game must be free from those rules to become genuinely new.

But they're not in opposition. They're two sides of the same bridge.

The Fund generates financial credibility and moves capital into the ecosystem in a language institutions understand. When a mainstream investor participates in the ReGen Civics Fund, they're stepping onto the bridge. The Game generates lived experience, community wisdom, and a growing body of proof that regenerative ways of organizing actually work. When someone completes their first quest, earns their first $ReGen, and participates in their first governance vote, they're walking toward the other side.

The long-term goal — the part of the work that feels most alive and most necessary — is building a bigger, better, more beautiful bridge. One that more people can find, trust, and cross. One that makes the journey from the old Game to the new Games feel less like a leap of faith and more like a clear, well-marked path.

We want the crossing to feel safe. We want it to feel joyful. We want it to feel, at least a little, like stepping into something extraordinary.

---

## Mastering Both Games Simultaneously

This is the strategic heart of ReGen Civics: mastering the economic systems of both the old Game and the new Games at the same time.

That means learning to speak the language of capital while building something that transcends it. It means creating governance structures that are legible to institutions and liberated from institutional logic. It means holding the tension between two very different sets of rules — not as a contradiction, but as the whole point.

Most regenerative movements have chosen one side. They've either built beautiful alternatives that can't access capital at scale, or they've worked within existing systems and found themselves gradually captured by that system's logic. We're trying to do something harder: hold both, simultaneously, without losing either.

It's ambitious. It's difficult. It requires exactly the kind of community intelligence, governance wisdom, and long-term thinking that the Game is designed to build.
