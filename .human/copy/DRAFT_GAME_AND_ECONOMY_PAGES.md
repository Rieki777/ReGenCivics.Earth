### Section 4: Three Tools for Coordinating a Society

Conventional societies coordinate with two main tools: markets and centralized governments. Markets are powerful but blind. They can't see drinkable rivers, breathable air, nourishing food, mental health, parenting, community care. These are "externalities." Governments were supposed to fill the gaps, but most have been captured by the market.

We need more coordination tools. SEEDS built three. ReGen Civics inherits and evolves all of them.

#### Tool 1: Contribution Scores

Every society needs a way to know who's contributing and how. In the dominant game, the primary metric is money earned. It doesn't matter how you earned it or whether it healed or harmed.

In our Game, we track a growing diversity of contributions: quests completed, people invited, land projects endorsed, transactions facilitated, proposals passed, forum engagement, events attended, food produced and distributed through local food networks.

Each action earns points. Points are ranked against all other players on a percentile scale from 0 to 99. Your score determines your share of the seasonal Harvest (new tokens distributed each cycle) and your governance voice.

The percentile system reverses inequality by design. If the wealthiest player has staked 100 million tokens and the second has 100 thousand, they both score 99 on staking. There's a ceiling. Hoarding produces diminishing returns. Contributing doesn't. Over time, wealth distributes outward. That's the opposite of every dominant economic system.

Scores decay over time. Active contributors earn more than people resting on history. Nobody is permanently locked out.

And the part that matters most: **what counts as a "contribution" is up to us.** The community decides, through governance, what to track, what to reward, and how to weight it. The current scores are the first draft. We evolve them together. If the community decides "hours spent teaching food preservation" should be tracked, we can build it. Now we actually can.

#### Tool 2: Gratitude

Some of the most valuable things people do are invisible to any tracking system. Caring for elders. Making art. Holding space after loss. Teaching for free because it needed sharing. Growing food for neighbors without charging.

Gratitude fills this gap. Each cycle (roughly 29.5 days, tied to lunar months), you receive a budget of gratitude tokens. Residents get 100, Citizens get 200. Send them to people for anything you're grateful for.

Holding gratitude does nothing. It activates when sent. Each acknowledgment sends 10% of your budget. Acknowledge more than 10 people, it divides proportionally.

At cycle's end, a pot of tokens (funded by the Harvest and community proposals) is distributed proportionally to the people who received the most gratitude. No invoice. No payment processing. Acknowledgment becomes economic value.

This is swarm intelligence for value distribution. The more people participating, the more accurate and decentralized it becomes. It creates a society where giving and being supported are compatible.

#### Tool 3: Proposals

Any citizen can create a proposal. Fund a land project. Adjust a harvest ratio. Create a new quest category. Change a contribution metric. Allocate resources to a bioregional food system. Approve a local food producer's application.

Proposals go to a vote. Citizens cast Trust Tokens (governance tokens earned through contribution). If it passes quorum and unity thresholds, it executes. Voice is proportional to contribution, not wealth.

The three tools together:

- Proposals for collective decisions (fund this project, change this rule)
- Contribution Scores for quantitative coordination (quests, transactions, invitations)
- Gratitude for qualitative coordination (care, art, teaching, presence)

Together they cover the full range of how humans create value for each other.

### Section 5: The Harvest

Every season, new tokens are created and distributed. This is the Harvest.

In conventional economies, new money is created by central banks as debt with interest, and distributed to banks. Value flows upward. In our economy, new tokens are created to match growth in economic activity. No debt. No interest. Distributed directly to:

- Residents and Citizens (based on contribution scores)
- Active regions and bioregions
- Qualifying organizations, including local food producers (based on regenerative reputation)
- A shared treasury governed by citizen proposals
- A reserve for long-term stability

Your share is determined by your contribution score. The more you contribute to the health of the planet, the community, and the economy, the more you receive. This is a Universal Earned Income: a direct share of the value created by the society you're helping build.

For local food producers, the Harvest has extra significance. Organizations that facilitate local food trade earn higher contribution multipliers. A food co-op earns tokens every time members transact through the system. The more regenerative the operation (rated by community members), the bigger the multiplier on rewards. The economy literally pays you to grow and distribute good food.

### Section 7: Reputation

Your reputation is built through participation: forum engagement, governance voting, peer vouching, following through on proposals. Scored on 0-99 percentile.

Reputation multiplies everything else. Score 99 = 2x multiplier. Score 68 = 1x. Below 7 = 0x, zeroing out all rewards.

This is the immune system. No central authority needed. A bad actor can have their rewards zeroed by losing community trust. Reputation decays over time ("composting"), prioritizing active contributors over those resting on past achievements.

---

### Section 8: Better Than Free

In the dominant economy, businesses pay 3-30% in transaction fees. Here, transactions are free. But it goes further: organizations earn contribution points for facilitating transactions. The more activity they support, the larger their Harvest share.

For a food co-op: accept tokens, facilitate community transactions, build regenerative reputation, earn a growing share of the Harvest. No fees extracted. Value flowing in instead of out. That's not altruism. That's better design.

For a local food delivery service: instead of taking 15-30% from restaurants and producers (like DoorDash, Uber Eats), you earn rewards from the economy for facilitating the transactions. Free for the producer. Free for the buyer. Better than free for you. This makes local food platforms that would be impossible in the extractive economy completely viable here.
