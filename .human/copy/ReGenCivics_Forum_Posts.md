## Topic: Investment & Finance

### Thread: How Money Moves in a Regenerative Economy
*Rieki Cordon · Author · March 4, 2026*

---

Most of us have been taught that money is neutral: that it just flows where returns are highest. But money is never neutral. It always reflects values. It always creates the world it touches.

ReGen Civics operates at two levels of the economy simultaneously, and understanding both changes how you see the whole project.

**Two economic systems, one mission**

The **ReGen Civics Fund** is rooted in the Dominant Game (the existing economic system) by design. It uses one of the old Game's most powerful coordination structures: a venture capital fund. This isn't a compromise. It's a strategy. To move serious capital into regenerative land projects, we need to speak the language of institutions and investors who live in the old Game. The Fund does that.

The **ReGen Civics Game** runs a parallel economy: one we're building together, rooted in contribution rather than capital. $ReGen tokens are earned through participation: completing quests, contributing to the community, stewarding the ecosystem. What $ReGen becomes is something we're co-creating: an in-game currency, a governance tool, a rewards system, a token whose utility reflects our shared values rather than market logic.

These two systems are designed to work together without collapsing into each other. The Fund is the bridge to the old world. The Game is the beginning of the new one.

**Here's what makes the Fund different:**

**Land-backed security.** The fund holds actual land: real, tangible, ecologically valuable land. Not paper promises. Not derivatives. Soil, water, food-forests, regenerative farms, and community infrastructure. This creates stability that financial markets can't manufacture.

**RCVoice governance.** Major decisions about how capital is deployed are governed by RCVoice tokens, earned through contributing to the Fund. The Fund uses a 90% Unity Model: consequential decisions require 90% agreement. No small group can redirect the fund. Real alignment has to be built.

**On-chain transparency via Hypha DAO.** Every significant transaction, vote, and decision is recorded on-chain. You can see exactly how the fund operates. No black boxes.

**Diversified across bioregions.** We're not betting on one geography or one project type. The fund spreads across ecosystems, cultures, and scales: creating resilience through diversity, exactly as nature does.

**Impact metrics via HEIST framework.** Returns aren't measured in financial yield alone. The HEIST framework tracks ecological and social outcomes: soil health, biodiversity, community wellbeing, economic resilience. Capital that improves these measures is capital working correctly.

**This thread is for real questions.** Ask about the investment thesis. Ask how the Fund and the Game's economies interact. Ask how returns work. Ask how impact is measured. Ask what happens when a project underperforms. Ask the uncomfortable things.

We believe in transparent conversation about money, because that's the only way to build a financial system that actually serves life.

## Topic: Governance & DAO

### Thread: You Have a Voice Here: Here's How to Use It
*Rieki Cordon · Author · March 5, 2026*

---

Most governance systems are designed to concentrate power. Committees, boards, executives: structures that funnel decision-making up the hierarchy and away from the people most affected and holding the most on-the-ground wisdom.

ReGen Civics is built on a different premise: that the people participating in an ecosystem are the ones best positioned to govern it.

But here's something important to understand first: **ReGen Civics has two distinct spaces, and each has its own governance.** Understanding why makes everything else clearer.

**Two spaces, two governance systems**

The **ReGen Civics Fund** is anchored in the Dominant Game (the existing economic and legal system) by design. It uses the coordination structure of a venture capital fund, and its governance is held by **RCVoice tokens**. RCVoice determines how the Fund's profits and distributions are allocated. This is serious capital governance: transparent, accountable, and legible to the institutions and investors who live in the old Game.

The **ReGen Civics Game** is anchored in the new Games we're building together. Its governance is held by **RGVoice tokens**, earned through genuine participation in the community and ecosystem. RGVoice shapes how the Game itself evolves: what quests exist, how the community grows, what we build together.

These two systems are separate by intention. The Fund needs to operate by rules the old Game recognizes. The Game is free to become something genuinely new.

**How RGVoice works:**

RGVoice is earned through participation: completing quests, contributing to the community, engaging with the ecosystem. The more genuinely you participate, the more your voice weighs in decisions. This isn't a plutocracy (governance by the wealthy) like most DAOs where you can just buy your way in. It's a contribution-weighted system that gives governance voice to those who actually show up.

**The 90% Unity Model:**

For major decisions, we require 90% agreement. Not simple majority. Not supermajority. 90%. This sounds radical, and it is. Consequential decisions can't be steamrolled by a slight majority. Genuine alignment has to be built. It creates a natural incentive to propose things people can actually get behind, and to keep refining proposals until they earn broad support, without requiring 100% consensus, which would give an extreme minority the ability to block all progress. As an open network, we don't filter membership the way 100%-consensus communities must.

**Seasonal Cycles:**

Our governance doesn't run on arbitrary quarterly timelines. Major reviews and decisions are aligned with equinoxes and solstices: natural rhythms that have oriented human communities for millennia. This isn't just symbolic. It's a commitment to living in time with the earth and attuning to the cycles that have always organized life.

**Hypha DAO Infrastructure:**

The technical backbone is Hypha DAO: tested since 2019, transparent, and built specifically for decentralized organizations with complex governance needs.

**What does this mean for you?**

Your participation in the Game earns RGVoice. That voice shapes what the Game becomes: what quests are created and verified, how resources flow, how the community evolves. It's real governance weight in a real DAO making real decisions.

The Fund is a different layer, governed separately by RCVoice, with its own accountability structure. Together, they form the full picture: one foot planted in the world that is, one foot in the world we're building.

This thread is for questions, proposals, critiques, and ideas. How do we make governance more accessible? More effective? More genuinely representative? This is where that conversation lives.

You're not a user here. You're a participant in the governance of a new kind of society. What does that mean to you?

What if changing the world felt less like a burden and more like an adventure?

That question is at the heart of how ReGen Civics is designed. We've borrowed the architecture of games (quests, rewards, seasons, progression) and applied it to something that actually matters: building regenerative civilization, together.

**Why quests?**

Because most of us know what needs to happen. We know the world needs more regenerative land projects, more aligned capital, more connected communities, more people who understand how ecosystems work. What's hard isn't the knowing. It's the doing. Quests give you a specific, meaningful action to take right now, with a community doing it alongside you, and real recognition when you complete it.

**What you earn:**

Completing a quest earns you **$ReGen tokens** and **RGVoice governance weight**. These aren't points in a closed system. $ReGen is a real token on the Base blockchain. RGVoice is real governance weight in a real DAO making real decisions about real resources. The game has actual stakes.

**How it works:**

1. Visit the **Quests** page on your profile (or find it under the quests menu)
2. Browse the available quests: we have a whole series called Welcome Aboard designed for people who are just getting started
3. Choose a quest that resonates with where you are right now
4. Complete the requirements (each quest tells you exactly what to do)
5. Submit proof of completion: usually a forum comment and a social post linking back

**The Welcome Aboard Quests** are the best starting point if you're new. Ten quests that root you in the platform, connect you with the community, and earn you real tokens while you learn. Each quest is designed to be genuinely meaningful, not just a checkbox.

**This thread is for everything quest-related:**

## Topic: Learning & Resources

### Thread: The Map Into This World: Resources That Have Shaped Us
*Rieki Cordon · Author · March 10, 2026*

---

Understanding how to build regenerative societies is a genuine intellectual challenge. It asks you to hold ecology, economics, governance, culture, spirituality, and systems thinking all at once, and then make it practical enough to actually do.

Nobody comes to this fully formed. We learn from each other, from the land, and from the thinkers who've been working these questions longest.

Here are some of the resources that have most shaped the thinking behind ReGen Civics:

**Books that changed how we see:**

*Foundational*

- *Sand Talk: How Indigenous Thinking Can Save the World* by Tyson Yunkaporta: Indigenous systems thinking that reframes how we understand knowledge, community, and time. Genuinely perspective-shifting.
- *The More Beautiful World Our Hearts Know Is Possible* by Charles Eisenstein: a quiet but radical book arguing that the old story of separation is ending and a new story of interconnectedness is beginning. One of the clearest articulations of why regenerative work is necessary and possible.
- *Braiding Sweetgrass* by Robin Wall Kimmerer: a botanist and member of the Citizen Potawatomi Nation weaves Indigenous plant wisdom with Western science. Teaches reciprocity, gratitude, and what it means to be truly in relationship with the living world.
- *The Ministry for the Future* by Kim Stanley Robinson: a near-future novel about the institutions, movements, and unlikely actors that might actually navigate climate crisis. More strategy document than fiction, and one of the most hopeful serious books about the future we've found.
- *Thinking, Fast and Slow* by Daniel Kahneman: a deep dive into the two systems that drive human decision-making. Essential for understanding why people don't always behave "rationally," and how to design governance and communication that works with human nature rather than against it.
- *The Surrender Experiment* by Michael Singer: a memoir about what happens when someone stops trying to control their life and simply says yes to what unfolds. Disarming and practical, it's the book most likely to change your inner game first.
- *Finite and Infinite Games* by James Carse: the philosophical foundation of the "infinite game" design underlying everything here. Short, mind-expanding, worth reading slowly.

*Social and Economic Design*

- *Doughnut Economics* by Kate Raworth: the clearest reframing of economics around planetary boundaries and human flourishing we've found. If you haven't read it, start here.
- *Sacred Economics* by Charles Eisenstein: traces the history of money and offers a vision of an economy rooted in gift, community, and ecological reality. Pairs beautifully with Doughnut Economics for understanding why the current system is broken and what might replace it.
- *The Dawn of Everything: A New History of Humanity* by David Graeber and David Wengrow: a sweeping reexamination of human history that dismantles the idea that hierarchy and capitalism are inevitable. Turns out, humans have organized themselves in radical variety for most of history.
- *Governing the Commons* by Elinor Ostrom: the Nobel Prize-winning study of how communities around the world have managed shared resources sustainably for generations without government control or private ownership. The practical foundation for many of the governance experiments in this space.
- *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* by David Graeber: traces debt through human history to show that money and markets are not natural phenomena but human inventions with specific and changeable logics. Changes how you see every transaction.
- *Non-Violent Communication: A Language of Life* by Marshall B. Rosenberg: a language framework for honest, compassionate expression and listening. Practical enough to use in every difficult conversation, deep enough to reshape how you understand human conflict entirely.
- *The Four Agreements* by Don Miguel Ruiz: a short, accessible distillation of Toltec wisdom into four practical agreements for living. Deceptively simple. Often transformative.
- *A Different Drum* by M. Scott Peck: a psychiatrist's honest exploration of what it actually takes to build real community among humans. Clear about the stages, the difficulty, and the depth of what's possible.
- *Creating a Life Together* by Diana Leafe Christian: the practical guide for people starting intentional communities and ecovillages. Grounded in real experience of what works and what doesn't.
- *Crucial Conversations* by Kerry Patterson, Joseph Grenny, Ron McMillan, and Al Switzler: tools for navigating high-stakes conversations when emotions run high and the stakes matter. Governance and community work runs on these conversations.
- *Dare to Lead* by Brené Brown: a research-backed framework for courageous leadership rooted in vulnerability, trust, and clear values. Reshapes what leadership looks like in non-hierarchical settings.
- *The Surprising Power of Liberating Structures* by Henri Lipmanowicz and Keith McCandless: 33 meeting and facilitation formats designed to unlock contribution and intelligence in groups. If you've sat in too many meetings where most people stay quiet, this book is the antidote.
- *Many Voices One Song: Shared Power with Sociocracy* by Jerry Koch-Gonzalez and Ted J. Rau: the clearest introduction to sociocracy as a governance method. Practical, values-grounded, and directly applicable to ReGen Civics governance design.
- *Change Here Now* by Adam Brock: a permaculture-informed guide to community resilience and personal transformation. Bridges inner and outer change in a way few books manage.

*Ecological Design*

- *The Web of Life* by Fritjof Capra: systems thinking made accessible. Understanding how living systems actually organize themselves changes how you think about governance, community, and land.
- *Designing Regenerative Cultures* by Daniel Christian Wahl: one of the broadest books in the space, tracing how systems thinking and ecological design can reshape culture, economics, governance, and education. A reference book as much as a read.
- *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* by David Holmgren: one of the foundational permaculture texts by one of its co-founders. Goes far beyond gardening into ethics, economics, and civilization design.
- *Biomimicry* by Janine Benyus: the book that launched a design movement. Shows how nature's 3.8 billion years of R&D offers solutions to almost every design challenge humans face.
- *Thinking in Systems: A Primer* by Donella Meadows: the most accessible introduction to systems thinking we've found. Understanding feedback loops, delays, and leverage points is foundational for anyone trying to change complex systems.
- *Mycelium Running* by Paul Stamets: a practical and visionary guide to working with fungi. Fungi as metaphor for networked intelligence, as soil-builder, as teacher. Essential for anyone working with land.
- *Regenerative Agriculture* by Richard Perkins: practical, visionary, and grounded in soil. Essential for anyone working with land.

*Organisational Design*

- *Reinventing Organizations* by Frédéric Laloux: the landmark study of organizations that have evolved beyond hierarchy and ego. Traces evolutionary stages of organizational consciousness, with detailed case studies of what those organizations actually look like in practice.
- *Better Work Together* edited by Susan Basterfield and Anthony Cabraal: practical wisdom from practitioners inside non-hierarchical organizations, covering conflict, roles, and culture. Grounded and honest.
- *We the People: Consenting to a Deeper Democracy* by John Buck and Sharon Villines: a guide to sociocratic principles and their application in organizations and communities. Pairs well with Many Voices One Song.
- *The Empowerment Manual* by Starhawk: a guide to building and sustaining horizontal organizing, drawing on decades of activist and community experience. Honest about the pitfalls and equipped with real tools.
- *Punished by Rewards* by Alfie Kohn: the research case against using external rewards to motivate people. Relevant for anyone designing contribution systems that aim to build intrinsic motivation rather than replace it.
- *Green Swans* by John Elkington: the author who coined "triple bottom line" revisits and extends the concept, arguing for systemic, generative capitalism that creates exponential positive change. Useful for connecting regenerative work to mainstream business language.
- *Exponential Organizations* by Salim Ismail, Michael Malone, and Yuri van Geest: a framework for understanding how new organizations scale dramatically by leveraging technology and community. Relevant context for thinking about how ReGen Civics grows.
- *The Regenerative Business* by Carol Sanford: argues that truly regenerative organizations develop the capacity of every person and living system they touch, rather than optimizing for output. Deep and challenging.
- *Radical Markets* by Eric Posner and Glen Weyl: two economists propose radical redesigns of property, voting, immigration, and antitrust that challenge both capitalism and socialism. Rigorous thought experiments for economic redesign.
- *More Equal Animals* by Dan Larimer: a short exploration of what true democracy might look like, written by one of the founders of decentralized blockchain governance. Direct, provocative, and relevant to DAO design.

*Human Design*

- *Atomic Habits* by James Clear: the practical guide to building habits that stick through small, consistent changes. Useful individually and relevant for thinking about how communities build healthy collective patterns.
- *Recapture the Rapture* by Jamie Wheal: a serious look at how humans can reliably access elevated states of consciousness, connection, and meaning through peak performance, flow states, and collective ritual. Relevant for how we design the Game experience.
- *Self Organisation: Counter-Economic Strategies* edited by Will Bradley, Mika Hannula, Cristina Ricupero, and Superflex: a collection exploring how artists, activists, and communities build autonomous economic structures outside the mainstream. More provocative than practical, but opens the imagination.

*Regenerative Movement Building*

- *The Infinite Game* by Simon Sinek: Sinek applies Carse's framework to business and leadership, arguing that organizations built to "win" eventually lose to those built to last and play indefinitely. A useful translation of Carse's philosophy into practical leadership.
- *Rules for Revolutionaries* by Becky Bond and Zack Exley: practical lessons on how big organizing (with real leadership distributed across thousands of volunteers) can change the scale of what's possible. Directly applicable to movement building.

**Talks and online resources:**

- **[The Power of Vulnerability](https://www.ted.com/talks/brene_brown_the_power_of_vulnerability)** by Brené Brown: the TED talk that introduced millions to the research on vulnerability and courage. Watch this before Dare to Lead.
- **[NVC Workshop with Marshall Rosenberg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nWb2B2uPfMo)**: a full workshop recording from the creator of Nonviolent Communication. The book is essential, but hearing Rosenberg work live with people changes how you understand it.
- **[Healing Trauma Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3Xi8vZSmBTRlD8Dnx6a16yeBTaSxKNdS)** with Gabor Maté: a deep, compassionate exploration of trauma, addiction, and what it actually takes for human beings to heal. If you're building communities and villages where people can truly thrive, this is foundational. Maté makes clear that so many of the social and ecological crises we're trying to address are downstream of unaddressed trauma, and that real regeneration has to include healing the inner landscape alongside the outer one. This is not optional context.
- **[Everything I Know](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/everything-i-know/)** by Buckminster Fuller: 42 hours of recorded sessions from 1975 in which Fuller distilled his life's thinking on design, systems, and the future. A landmark archive for anyone working at the intersection of design and civilization.
- **[decentpatterns.xyz/library](https://decentpatterns.xyz/library/)**: a library of design patterns for decentralized systems. Useful for governance and coordination design.

**Online and active:**

- **explore.joinseeds.earth**: the ReGen Civics knowledge base. Start here for a map of the whole ecosystem.
- **The HEIST Framework**: our approach to measuring ecological and social impact in terms that matter.
- **The Foundational Series**: our video series introducing the ReGen Civics game and vision. Watch before diving in: [youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3Xi8vZSmBTStS0BoFItW8389HJLypX0E](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3Xi8vZSmBTStS0BoFItW8389HJLypX0E&si=JUaZESvGBP4Cvg2N)

**This list is just the beginning.**

The collective intelligence in this community is enormous. What have you read, watched, or encountered that cracked something open for you? What's the resource you keep recommending to people? What's the question you're currently trying to find better answers to?
