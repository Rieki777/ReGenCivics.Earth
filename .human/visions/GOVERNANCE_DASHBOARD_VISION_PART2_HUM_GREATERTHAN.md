## What The Hum and Greaterthan Teach Us That SEEDS Missed

SEEDS built impressive economic infrastructure and governance contracts. What they never built was the *human layer*: the facilitation patterns, the conflict resolution processes, the money stories, the onboarding rituals, the rhythms that make a distributed community actually function as a community.

The Hum (founded by Richard D. Bartlett, co-founder of Loomio) and Greaterthan (stewards of Cobudget, born from Enspiral) have spent a decade figuring out exactly this. Their work addresses the gap between "we have governance tools" and "people actually use them well together."

This is the missing piece. ReGen Civics has the game design, the fund structure, the token economics, and now the governance pipeline. What follows are 50 ideas for the human infrastructure that makes all of it work.

## Research Summary

### The Hum
Founded 2016 by Richard D. Bartlett (co-founder of Loomio and Enspiral). Consultancy for decentralized teams. Core offering: the Four Collaboration Rhythms (Connect, Plan, Work, Reflect) and the Four Decision-Making Methods (Consensus, Consent, Advice Process, Mandate). They teach that mature organizations use multiple decision methods and switch between them based on context. Key frameworks: Team User Manuals, Microsolidarity (concentric circles of community), and the power dynamics work (making implicit power explicit).

### Greaterthan
Founded 2017 from an Enspiral gathering. Cooperative stewarding Cobudget (participatory budgeting software). Core innovation: "Happy Money Stories" where team members share narratives about their work before splitting income, and Cobudgeting where individuals allocate their portion of a shared fund to proposals they support. Key principle: "Entrepreneurship = Decision-Making Power" (those doing the work decide about it). Three membership tiers: Partners, Associates, Explorers. Living handbook documenting all agreements. Academy teaching Liberating Structures, self-management, network governance.

### Local Scale
Public benefit organization building bioregional economies through blockchain. Three-tier membership (Visitor, Resident, Citizen) matching SEEDS/ReGen Civics. LETS module for local exchange. Integration with Hypha DAO for bioregional ambassadors to register local digital currencies. oSwaps protocol for inter-bioregional trade. Built on Cosmos SDK with IBC for cross-chain interoperability. This is the food-based local economy layer Rye described.

### Mutual Credit and Food-Backed Currency Precedents
Grassroots Economics (Kenya): Community Inclusion Currencies reaching 30K users. SARDEX (Sardinia): B2B mutual credit, 4K+ businesses, ~50M EUR annual trade. BerkShares: operating since 2006, hundreds of businesses. Mendo Credits: food-backed local currency. Trustlines Protocol: open-source peer-to-peer credit. ReSource Protocol: dual-currency model for regenerative economies.

## 50 Ideas from The Hum, Greaterthan, and Local Scale (ideas 106-155)

### Collaboration Rhythms for Governance (from The Hum)

**106. Four-beat governance rhythm built into the seasonal calendar.** Each season follows the Hum's four beats: Connect (first two weeks: community gathering, relationship building, gratitude round), Plan (week 3-4: proposal creation, discussion, bioregion alignment), Work (weeks 5-10: execution, quest completion, land project milestones), Reflect (final two weeks: retrospective, harvest distribution, contribution scoring). The dashboard shows which beat the community is in. Every proposal template auto-suggests timing based on the current beat.

**107. Team User Manuals for land projects.** Each land project fills out a "Project User Manual": how they communicate, what decisions they make locally vs bring to the network, what support they need, their seasonal rhythms, their soil type and climate challenges, what makes them unique. Other projects read these before collaborating. Stored on the dashboard. Surfaces compatibility between projects considering resource sharing or knowledge exchange.

**108. Microsolidarity circles as governance units.** The Hum's concentric circle model: Self (personal practice), Crew (3-6 people, high trust), Congregation (15-150, shared purpose), Network (150+, weak ties). ReGen Civics maps these to: Player (individual), Pod (3-6 players doing a quest chain together), Bioregion (15-150 players in a watershed), Network (all bioregions). Governance decisions flow through these layers. Pods discuss, Bioregions deliberate, Network ratifies. The dashboard shows which layer a proposal lives at.

**109. "Never govern alone" facilitation pattern.** Borrowed from The Hum's "never host alone" principle. Every governance proposal has two shepherds: one handles logistics (timing, quorum tracking, notification sending) and one handles the human side (facilitating discussion, surfacing concerns, bridging perspectives). The dashboard assigns shepherd pairs from active Stewards.

**110. Dynamic roles instead of fixed positions.** The Hum teaches that self-organizing teams use roles that shift based on need, not fixed job titles. ReGen Civics governance roles (proposal shepherd, bioregion steward, fund council member, storyteller) should be dynamic: held for a season, reviewed at the Reflect beat, and rotated. The dashboard shows current role holders and when roles next rotate. Anyone can express interest in a role during the Plan beat.

**111. Explicit power mapping on the dashboard.** The Hum's core teaching: make implicit power visible. The dashboard shows: who holds delegation from the most players, who has the highest contribution score, who has shepherded the most proposals, who controls treasury keys. Power isn't hidden. It's visible, accountable, and challengeable. This prevents the "tyranny of structurelessness" that kills decentralized orgs.

**112. Connect rituals before every major vote.** Before any network-wide governance vote opens, there's a mandatory Connect ritual: a facilitated call (or async thread) where the proposer shares the human story behind the proposal, affected parties share their perspectives, and the community does a round of acknowledgment. Not a debate. A listening session. The dashboard tracks which proposals have completed their Connect ritual before allowing the vote to open.

**113. Conflict transformation protocol.** The Hum teaches that conflict is inevitable and valuable. ReGen Civics needs a published conflict transformation protocol on the dashboard: Step 1, the two parties have a direct conversation (optionally with a facilitator). Step 2, if unresolved, a peer mediation circle of 3-5 trusted community members. Step 3, if still unresolved, a governance proposal to the bioregion. Step 4, if still unresolved, a network-level restorative justice process. The dashboard tracks active conflicts (anonymized) and resolution rates.

**114. Group norms co-creation for each bioregion.** The Hum's group norms exercise: each bioregion creates its own explicit norms for communication, decision-making, and conflict handling. These norms are documented on the dashboard, revisited at the start of each season, and amendable by consent. A bioregion in the Pacific Northwest might have different norms than one in Southern Appalachia. Local culture matters.

**115. Decision-making method selector.** The Hum's four methods (Consensus, Consent, Advice Process, Mandate) should be selectable per proposal on the dashboard. The default is Consent (good enough for now, safe enough to try). But the proposer can request Consensus for high-stakes decisions, Advice Process for operational choices, or Mandate for urgent actions within a steward's domain. The selected method changes the voting UI, quorum requirements, and approval criteria.

### Participatory Finance (from Greaterthan)

**116. Cobudgeting for fund allocation.** This is the big one. Instead of a centralized committee deciding which land projects get funded, use Greaterthan's Cobudget model. Each season, the fund's allocation pool is divided among all Citizens based on contribution score. Each Citizen then allocates their portion to the land project proposals ("buckets") they believe in. No consensus required. No committee bottleneck. Distributed decision-making over real money. The dashboard shows each bucket's funding progress in real time.

**117. Happy Money Stories for seasonal harvest.** Greaterthan's practice: before splitting income, each person tells the story of their contribution. ReGen Civics adapts this for seasonal harvest distribution. Before governance tokens are distributed, each player (or at least each Steward and active Citizen) shares a short "harvest story" on the forum: what they contributed, what they learned, what challenged them. These stories are attached to the contribution score calculation. The community reads them before the harvest distribution finalizes. Money flows to people whose stories demonstrate genuine impact.

**118. Transparent financial dashboard (open books).** Greaterthan's core principle: money transparency is power transparency. The governance dashboard shows everything: fund AUM, treasury balance, disbursements by land project, governance token distribution, gratitude flows, seasonal expenses, steward compensation, operational costs. Open books. Every dollar tracked. Every token accounted for. This builds trust faster than any marketing campaign.

**119. Advice Process for sub-threshold decisions.** Greaterthan uses the Advice Process for decisions under 1,500 EUR. ReGen Civics should set a threshold (say, $5,000 or equivalent in tokens). Below the threshold, any Citizen or Steward can make the decision after consulting affected parties and posting the decision on the forum for 48 hours. Above the threshold, full governance vote required. This prevents governance gridlock on small operational decisions while maintaining accountability.

**120. Money stories workshop as an onboarding quest.** Greaterthan teaches that personal "money stories" (beliefs, fears, shame, aspirations around money) shape how people participate in economic governance. ReGen Civics should include a "Money Stories" quest in the onboarding chain: a guided reflection where new players explore their relationship with money, share (optionally) with their pod, and learn how participatory budgeting works. This demystifies the economic governance layer before players encounter it.

**121. Needs-based distribution experiments.** Greaterthan documented Centrifuge's 6-month experiment with needs-based money distribution (allocating based on need rather than work performed). ReGen Civics should enable bioregions to experiment with alternative distribution models. A bioregion could vote to run a season where harvest distribution factors in need (land projects in ecological crisis get priority) alongside contribution. The dashboard tracks the experiment and its outcomes.

**122. Principle of reversibility for governance decisions.** Greaterthan's governance framework weights decisions by how easily they can be undone. The dashboard should auto-categorize proposals: easily reversible (operational decisions, quest parameters, UI changes) get lightweight governance (Advice Process or Consent with 3-day window). Hard to reverse (fund disbursements, constitutional amendments, membership criteria changes) get heavy governance (Consensus with 14-day window and higher quorum). The weight is visible on each proposal card.

**123. Three-tier involvement model.** Greaterthan has Partners, Associates, and Explorers. ReGen Civics already has Visitor/Resident/Citizen/Steward. The insight is that involvement is fluid. A Steward going through a difficult season should be able to step down to Citizen-level involvement without shame or penalty, then step back up when ready. The dashboard shows current involvement level and allows self-adjustment. Life happens. Governance should accommodate that.

### Community Infrastructure (from The Hum + Greaterthan combined)

**124. Living handbook as a governance artifact.** Greaterthan maintains a public handbook documenting all their agreements. ReGen Civics should have a living governance handbook on the dashboard: decision-making agreements, financial agreements, people agreements, conflict protocols, bioregion norms, constitutional principles. Version-controlled. Amendable by governance vote. Every change tracked. New players read it during onboarding. It's the constitution, operating manual, and cultural guide in one document.

**125. Liberating Structures for bioregion gatherings.** Greaterthan teaches 33 group facilitation methods called Liberating Structures. The dashboard should include a "Facilitation Toolkit" section with templates for bioregion gatherings: 1-2-4-All (individual reflection, pair discussion, small group, whole group), Troika Consulting (peer consultation in groups of 3), 15% Solutions (what can each person do right now without permission), and others. Each template includes timing, instructions, and a digital facilitation guide.

**126. Onboarding as ritual, not just tutorial.** The Hum teaches that onboarding includes knowledge transfer, social integration, and ritualized celebration. When a new player joins ReGen Civics: (1) they get a welcome message from a real player in their bioregion (not automated), (2) they're invited to a pod, (3) they complete the first quest with their pod, and (4) their name and photo appear on the bioregion dashboard with a "new member" badge for their first season. The ritual of being seen and welcomed matters.

**127. Retrospective protocol for every season.** The Hum's Reflect beat, formalized. At the end of every season, each bioregion runs a structured retrospective: What worked? What didn't? What will we change? What are we grateful for? The dashboard provides the template, collects responses, and aggregates insights network-wide. The network-level retrospective becomes a published seasonal report that investors, partners, and the public can read.

**128. Peer coaching circles for stewards.** The Hum runs coaching circles where leaders work through real challenges with peer support. ReGen Civics Stewards should have monthly peer coaching circles: 4-6 stewards from different bioregions meet (video or async) to discuss governance challenges, share practices, and support each other. The dashboard facilitates scheduling and provides reflection prompts.

**129. Shared facilitation rotation.** Greaterthan's principle: facilitation is shared, not one person's job. Every governance meeting, every bioregion gathering, every seasonal festival has facilitation rotated among trained participants. The dashboard tracks who has facilitated recently and suggests the next facilitator. A "Facilitation Training" quest teaches the basics. Over time, everyone in the community can facilitate.

**130. Trauma-informed governance practices.** Greaterthan offers trauma-informed collaboration training. ReGen Civics is working with communities healing from extractive systems, displacement, and environmental grief. Governance should accommodate this: longer response times for sensitive proposals, anonymous feedback options, content warnings on discussions about land loss or economic hardship, and trained facilitators available for high-emotion discussions. The dashboard includes a "community care" section with resources.

### Bioregional Economics (from Local Scale + Mutual Credit Research)

**131. Local Scale integration for bioregional currencies.** Local Scale already does what Rye described: bioregional ambassadors register local digital currencies, create DAOs for local governance, and use the oSwaps protocol for inter-bioregional trade. ReGen Civics should integrate with Local Scale rather than rebuilding this from scratch. The governance dashboard shows the bioregion's local currency alongside $ReGen and $RCivics. Players can swap between them.

**132. LETS module for each bioregion.** Local Exchange Trading Systems let community members trade goods and services using a local unit of account without needing cash. Each bioregion on the dashboard gets a LETS marketplace: farmers list produce, builders list labor hours, healers list sessions, land projects list volunteer needs. Trades settle in the bioregion's local currency. The global $ReGen token provides interoperability between bioregions.

**133. Food-backed currency option for bioregions.** Following the Mendo Credits model: a bioregion with sufficient food production can back its local currency with reserves of stored food. The dashboard tracks the food reserve (reported by participating farms), the currency in circulation, and the exchange rate. Players in that bioregion can redeem local tokens for actual food. This is the bridge from digital governance to physical sustenance.

**134. Mutual credit system for land projects.** From SARDEX and Grassroots Economics: land projects within a bioregion can extend credit to each other. Project A has excess lumber. Project B needs lumber but has excess seedlings. They trade using mutual credit, with the bioregion's local currency as the unit of account. No money changes hands. The dashboard tracks credit balances and ensures no project exceeds its credit limit (set by governance vote based on the project's asset base).

**135. Bioregional economic health indicators.** From the SEEDS dashboard, evolved with Local Scale data. Each bioregion shows: local currency circulation velocity, trade volume between member projects, food production metrics, local employment supported, import replacement ratio (how much the bioregion produces locally vs imports), and economic diversity index. These feed into the doughnut economics visualization from idea #94.

**136. oSwaps for inter-bioregional trade.** When a land project in the Pacific Northwest has something a project in the Great Lakes needs, they trade through the oSwaps protocol. The governance dashboard shows inter-bioregional trade flows on the network map. Active trade routes between bioregions are highlighted. The more bioregions trade with each other, the stronger the network economy.

**137. Bioregion-customizable currency parameters.** Each bioregion's local currency can have its own parameters set by local governance: demurrage rate (negative interest that encourages spending rather than hoarding), credit limits, trade fees, and redemption rules. These parameters are visible on the bioregion's dashboard section and amendable by the bioregion's governance vote. Global $ReGen provides stability. Local currencies provide flexibility.

**138. "Import replacement" tracking.** From the Schumacher Center's bioregional economics: track what each bioregion currently imports and identify opportunities for local production. The dashboard shows: "Your bioregion imports 80% of its food. Three active land projects produce vegetables, grains, and herbs. A new dairy project would reduce food imports by 15%." This guides fund allocation and incubator recruitment toward filling actual economic gaps.

### Game Design for Governance (synthesis)

**139. Governance as a quest chain.** The governance participation pathway is itself a quest chain: Quest 1 (read the governance handbook), Quest 2 (observe a governance vote and post a reflection), Quest 3 (delegate your votes to a steward and explain why), Quest 4 (comment on an active proposal), Quest 5 (submit your first proposal using the Advice Process), Quest 6 (shepherd a proposal through the full governance cycle). Completing this chain is a requirement for reaching Citizen tier.

**140. Facilitation skill tree.** A branching skill tree visible on the player profile: basic facilitation (run a check-in round), intermediate facilitation (facilitate a consent decision), advanced facilitation (mediate a conflict), master facilitation (design and run a seasonal festival). Each level is a quest with real-world practice requirements. The dashboard shows the community's facilitation capacity: how many trained facilitators per bioregion.

**141. Governance XP that decays without participation.** Governance tokens earned through contribution should slowly decay if a player stops participating. Not a punishment. A natural cycle. Like a garden that needs tending. If you haven't voted, commented, or contributed in 60 days, your governance weight starts reducing. Active participation keeps it alive. This prevents ghost voters and governance capture by inactive accounts.

**142. "Propose a Dashboard Upgrade" as a permanent quest.** The governable dashboard from idea #91, gamified. A standing quest that's always available: "Propose a Dashboard Upgrade." Complete it by submitting a proposal for a new metric, visualization, or feature. If the community votes to adopt it, you earn a significant governance token bonus and a "Dashboard Architect" badge. The best proposals get built. The community shapes its own tools.

**143. Seasonal festival as the capstone governance event.** Each season ends with a festival: an in-person or hybrid gathering where harvest stories are told, governance decisions are celebrated (or mourned), contribution scores are honored, new stewards are installed, and the next season's direction is set. The dashboard counts down to the festival, shows the agenda (built by governance proposals), and tracks RSVPs. The festival is where the digital and physical layers of the movement meet.

### Welcome Experience and Platform Philosophy

**144. "Powered by a suite of interoperable tools" tooltip.** Instead of just "Powered by Loomio," the footer shows: "ReGen Gov. Powered by a suite of interoperable tools." Hovering or clicking reveals: Loomio (deliberation), SEEDS Passport (economic dashboard inspiration), Hypha (on-chain governance on Base), Local Scale (bioregional currencies), Cobudget (participatory fund allocation), and the wisdom of The Hum, Greaterthan, Enspiral, and the network state movement. Credit the lineage.

**145. First-visit welcome popup: the full story.** When a player first visits gov.regencivics.earth, a welcome modal tells the story (see full design spec below). Not a tutorial. Not a feature list. The story of what this is, why it exists, and what they can do. Ends with two buttons: "Enter the Game" and "Explore the Dashboard."

**146. Bioregion auto-population from main site.** Since accounts are shared between regencivics.earth and gov.regencivics.earth, the dashboard auto-populates the player's bioregion. If they haven't joined one, a prominent "Join a Bioregion" card appears in the bioregion section with a map showing available bioregions and their current membership counts. Joining is one click. The existing bioregion options from the main site populate the choices.

**147. Bioregion boundary proposals.** Players can propose altering bioregion boundaries or creating new bioregions. The proposal includes a map drawing tool, a justification (why existing boundaries don't work), and requires signatures from 5+ players in the affected area. The dashboard shows the proposed boundary change overlaid on the current map. The affected bioregion(s) vote on the change.

**148. Active proposal stream on the dashboard.** The economic metrics share the dashboard with a live stream of active governance proposals. Each proposal card shows: title, category (fund allocation, policy, dashboard upgrade, bioregion), decision method (consent, advice, etc.), time remaining, current vote tally, and a one-line summary. Players can vote directly from the dashboard without navigating away.

**149. Bioregion section with depth.** Each player's dashboard shows their bioregion section: local currency balance, active land projects, upcoming events, open governance proposals, bioregion health indicators, fellow members online, and recent gratitude flows within the bioregion. It's a mini-dashboard within the dashboard. Bioregion stewards can pin announcements to the top.

**150. "Integrate Your Tool" incentive program.** The welcome popup mentions earning $ReGen tokens for integrating tools. Formalize this: developers, organizations, and tool builders who integrate their tools into the ReGen Gov stack earn $ReGen tokens. The governance dashboard has an "Integrations" section showing connected tools and a proposal template for new integrations. The community votes on which integrations get funded.

**151. Interoperability as a core dashboard metric.** The census dashboard tracks interoperability: how many external tools are connected, how many bioregional currencies are active, how many cross-network credential verifications have happened, how many inter-bioregional trades completed. Interoperability isn't a feature. It's a metric the community actively governs and grows.

**152. Progressive dashboard reveal tied to citizenship.** Visitors see: the welcome story, the census metrics, and "Enter the Game." Residents see: their bioregion, active proposals they can comment on, the economic overview. Citizens see: full economic dashboard, voting interface, delegation controls, cobudgeting. Stewards see: all of the above plus treasury views, power mapping, facilitation tools, and admin controls. Nobody is overwhelmed. Everyone sees what's relevant to their current level of engagement.

**153. "Standing on the shoulders of" credits page.** A dedicated page honoring the lineage: SEEDS (the first passport for a regenerative economy), Loomio (deliberation infrastructure), Hypha (on-chain governance), Local Scale (bioregional economies), The Hum (collaboration patterns), Greaterthan (participatory finance), Enspiral (the network that proved it could work), the Schumacher Center (bioregional economics since 1980), Kate Raworth (doughnut economics), Balaji Srinivasan (network state thesis), and every land-based community that has practiced regenerative governance for centuries before any of this was digital.

**154. The governance dashboard as a "passport."** Reframe the entire dashboard as a digital passport for the regenerative civilization. Your profile page is your passport: citizenship tier, bioregion, contribution history, credentials earned, governance participation record, economic activity. When you interact with other regenerative networks (Hypha, Regen Network, Local Scale), your passport travels with you as verifiable credentials. The word "passport" connects to SEEDS' original vision but executes it at a higher level.

**155. "Heal the land, heal ourselves" as the dashboard's north star.** Every time the dashboard loads, the heading reads: "Heal the land, heal ourselves." Below it, the living census shows how the movement is doing against that single moral commitment. Ecological metrics (land restored, soil health improved, biodiversity increased) on one side. Human metrics (players supported, gratitude exchanged, conflicts resolved, governance participation) on the other. Two sides of the same healing. Visible every time you open the dashboard.

## The Welcome Experience: Full Design Spec

### First Visit: Welcome Modal

When a player first visits gov.regencivics.earth (detected via a `hasSeenWelcome` flag on their account):

**Phase 1: The Story (full-screen modal, dark forest background, glass-panel card)**

> **Welcome to ReGen Gov**
>
> You've entered the governance layer of the Regenerative Renaissance.
>
> What you see here is powered by a suite of interoperable tools built by movements that came before us and the ones we're building alongside. The base is Loomio for deliberation, blended with the SEEDS Passport's economic vision, wisdom from The Hum, Greaterthan, Enspiral, and decentralized organizing communities across the world.
>
> It's interoperable with Hypha for token issuance on Base blockchain and secure on-chain governance decisions. And with Local Scale for using those tokens to co-create regenerative bioregional economic systems based on food, where we can build our own financial systems backed by real food systems.
>
> $ReGen is a global currency. But we help bioregions set up their own currencies and alternative economic systems that become interoperable with other bioregions, land projects, and our growing borderless digital economy.
>
> This dashboard tracks the health of our movement. And the dashboard itself is governed by you. Propose changes. Vote on what we track. Shape the tools that shape the civilization.
>
> **[Enter the Game]** **[Explore the Dashboard]**
>
> *Integrate your tools, earn $ReGen tokens. [Learn more]*

Design notes:
- The text fades in paragraph by paragraph (subtle, not flashy)
- "Enter the Game" is the primary CTA (green, pill-shaped, prominent)
- "Explore the Dashboard" is secondary (outlined, smaller)
- "Integrate your tools" is a subtle link at the bottom
- The modal is dismissible with a close button but not auto-dismissing
- On mobile, it's a full-screen card. On desktop, it's a centered modal over a blurred dashboard preview

## How All of This Connects

The 155 ideas across both documents form a coherent architecture:

**Layer 1: Human Infrastructure** (The Hum + Greaterthan)
- Collaboration rhythms, facilitation patterns, conflict protocols, money stories, team user manuals, microsolidarity circles, living handbook. This is the culture layer. Without it, tools are empty.

**Layer 2: Governance Mechanics** (Network State + SEEDS + Loomio)
- Bicameral chambers, conviction voting, liquid delegation, rage-quit, cobudgeting, consent decisions, proposal templates, seasonal rhythm. This is the decision-making layer.

**Layer 3: Economic Infrastructure** (SEEDS + Local Scale + Mutual Credit)
- Token economics, harvest cycles, escrow disbursements, peer-to-peer exchange, bioregional currencies, food-backed money, inter-bioregional trade. This is the value flow layer.

**Layer 4: Identity and Progression** (Network State + SEEDS + Game Design)
- Progressive citizenship, SBTs, verifiable credentials, contribution mining, quest chains, skill trees, governance XP. This is the participation layer.

**Layer 5: The Dashboard** (all of the above, made visible)
- The governance dashboard is where all four layers become tangible. Every metric, every proposal, every bioregion, every quest, every token flow is visible here. And the dashboard itself is governed by the community that uses it.
