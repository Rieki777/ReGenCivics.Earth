# ReGen Civics Governance

## Dual Governance Systems

ReGen Civics operates two distinct but complementary governance systems:

### 1. **ReGen Civics Fund Governance** - Institutional Decision-Making

The Fund is governed by a Council of Domain Experts and four voice-holder groups making strategic decisions about capital allocation, land project selection, and alliance partnerships.

### 2. **ReGen Game Governance** - Player-Driven Evolution

The Game is governed entirely by players through earned voice, ensuring active participation shapes the game's direction and rules.

---

## Two Tokens, Two Powers

ReGen Civics uses two distinct tokens, each serving a fundamentally different purpose. Understanding the difference is key to understanding how the ecosystem works.

### **RCVoice** - Governance Token

RCVoice is your voice in fund and game decisions. It is earned through active participation and contribution, and wanes by 3% each month to ensure governance always remains with those who are currently active. RCVoice governs decisions: who gets funded, how the game evolves, what proposals pass.

### **$RCivics** - Rewards Token

$RCivics is your claim on fund returns and rewards. When the portfolio generates returns - from Land Projects, Alliance Organizations, or other fund activities - distributions flow proportionally to $RCivics token holders. $RCivics distributes rewards: it is a stake in the abundance the network creates together.

**The key distinction:** Voice governs. Tokens distribute. These two systems are designed to be complementary - active contributors earn governance power through participation, while long-term stakeholders hold a proportional claim on the value the network generates.

## ReGen Civics Fund Governance

### The Four Voice-Holder Groups

The ReGen Civics Fund is governed by four distinct groups, each bringing unique perspectives and expertise:

#### 1. **Council of Domain Experts**

- **Role**: Operational leadership and strategic guidance

- **Responsibilities**:
  - Evaluate land project applications for funding
  - Assess alliance partner fit, impact, and deal structure
  - Guide fund strategy, risk management, and draft seasonal funding proposals
  - Mentor emerging leaders in the regenerative movement

- **Voice Share (Phase 1)**: 40%

- **Selection**: Domain experts selected by all voice holders

#### 2. **Land Projects**

- **Role**: Ground-truth perspective on regenerative implementation

- **Responsibilities**:
  - Provide feedback on fund mechanisms and support structures
  - Share learnings from on-the-ground implementation
  - Propose new alliance partnerships and collaborations
  - Vote on seasonal fund priorities

- **Voice Share (Phase 1)**: 20%

- **Selection**: One representative per active land project in the fund

#### 3. **Alliance Organizations**

- **Role**: Ecosystem integration and movement building

Alliance Organizations are a critical part of the network's economic design. They provide the specialized services that Land Projects need - from regenerative agriculture expertise to legal, financial, and infrastructure support. By prioritizing Alliance Organizations as service providers, Land Projects keep resources circulating within the regenerative network rather than flowing out to conventional service providers. This internal circulation strengthens the whole ecosystem.

- **Responsibilities**:
  - Connect fund to broader regenerative networks
  - Propose collaborative initiatives and partnerships
  - Provide specialized expertise (agriculture, finance, infrastructure, governance, etc.)
  - Deliver services to Land Projects, keeping resources within the network
  - Vote on fund direction and new opportunities

- **Voice Share (Phase 1)**: 20%

- **Selection**: One representative per active alliance partner

#### 4. **Investors**

- **Role**: Capital stewardship and financial accountability

- **Responsibilities**:
  - Ensure fund sustainability and ROI on regenerative impact
  - Propose investment strategies and risk management
  - Vote on capital allocation and fund priorities
  - Share insights on market opportunities

- **Voice Share (Phase 1)**: 20%

- **Selection**: Proportional to capital contributed (with minimum thresholds)

### Governance Evolution: Three Phases

The fund's governance intentionally evolves to include "The People" - those living and working in land projects and organizations:

#### **Phase 1: Birth (Current)**

- **The People**: 0% voice

- **Council: 40%**

- **Other Groups**: 20% each (Land Projects, Alliance Orgs, Investors)

- **Focus**: Establishing fund infrastructure, proving model, building trust

#### **Phase 2: Adolescence**

- **The People**: 1% voice

- **Council: 39%**

- **Other Groups**: 20% each

- **Focus**: Increasing community participation, testing distributed governance

#### **Phase 3: Maturity**

- **The People**: 50% voice

- **Other Groups**: ~12.5% each (each group high enough to hold veto-power)

- **Focus**: Full community co-governance, distributed decision-making

### Seasonal Voting Process

Each Season the Council will create a proposal to bring to the Seasonal Festival 4 weeks leading up to the festival:

1. **Pre-Proposal Phase**: Any voice-holder can propose initiatives, policy changes, or capital allocations to the council at any time.

1. **Discussion Phase (Weeks 1-4)**: Community discusses proposals, refines details, builds consensus

1. **Voting Phase (Week 5 during Festival)**: Voice-holders vote on proposals and funds move immediately if proposal passes.

1. **Implementation Phase (Week 6+): Do the work!**

**Voting Threshold**: 90% approval required for major decisions (fund strategy, distributions, governance changes)

### Rewards and Fee Distribution

ReGen Civics distinguishes between two types of distributions, each governed by different rules:

#### **Portfolio Returns and Success Fees**

When the portfolio generates returns - from Land Projects delivering regenerative outcomes, Alliance Organizations generating revenue through their services, or other fund activities - success fees are distributed proportionally to **$RCivics token holders**. This means your claim on rewards is determined by your token holdings, not your governance voice. The two are intentionally separate: you can hold tokens without having active voice, and you can earn voice without holding tokens.

This design aligns long-term stakeholders with the fund's success while keeping governance responsive to active participants.

#### **Management Fees**

Management fees are distributed as follows:

- **50% to the Council of Domain Experts**: Reflecting the Council's central role in operational leadership, project evaluation, strategic guidance, and day-to-day fund management. A portion of Council fees are allocated to pay out worker proposals to ReGen Civics contributors, ensuring the people doing the work are compensated from within the network.

- **50% distributed proportionally to all voice holders**: Distributed in proportion to RCVoice holdings across all four groups (Council, Land Projects, Alliance Orgs, Investors). This means the Council receives the majority of total management fees when both portions are combined, reflecting their outsized contribution to fund operations.

This structure aligns incentives: the Council is rewarded for the work they do, while all voice-holders share in the fund's operational success.

#### **Keeping Resources in the Network**

A core design principle of ReGen Civics is that resources should circulate within the regenerative network wherever possible. When Land Projects need services - legal, agricultural, financial, technical, or otherwise - they are encouraged to source those services from Alliance Organizations within the network. This keeps capital flowing between regenerative actors rather than leaking out to conventional service providers. Alliance Organizations generate revenue by serving Land Projects, and that revenue flows back into the ecosystem through $RCivics distributions and continued investment in the network.

## ReGen Game: By the Players, For the Players

The ReGen Game operates on a fundamentally different principle: **governance by active participation**. Players earn voice through Quest completion and contributions, ensuring that those who are most engaged in the Game's mission have the greatest say in its direction.

### How Player Voice Works

#### **Earn Voice Through Action**

Every quest completed = voice earned. Every contribution made = voice earned. The more you play and contribute, the more your voice grows. There's no minimum threshold; everyone who participates has a voice.

#### **Monthly Voice Decay (3%)**

Voice wanes by 3% each month. This ensures the game continuously shifts governance power to current active players. It doesn't matter if you joined a few years into the game; your voice grows with your participation and the game always belongs to those playing now.

#### **Governance Through Growth**

Quests are designed to increase your understanding, health, capability, awareness, and decision-making abilities. The more you play, the better equipped you are to make wise decisions about the Game's direction. Our thesis: the Game should be governed by those actively working on themselves.

### Why?

#### **Decentralized by Design**

No single player or group can dominate governance. Voice is earned through participation and naturally redistributes through monthly waning. The Game remains decentralized and responsive to the active community.

#### **Meritocratic & Inclusive**

There's no gatekeeping or voting committee. Anyone can participate and earn voice. New players can quickly gain influence by engaging deeply with quests and contributing to the community.

#### **Aligned with Regeneration**

Governance power flows to those who are most committed to personal and collective regeneration. The Game's direction is shaped by players who are actively healing themselves and contributing to the movement.

#### **Future-Proof**

The monthly voice decay ensures the Game never becomes stale or controlled by early players. As new seasons begin and new players join, the Game continuously refreshes its governance. It's always a Game for active players.

### Game Governance in Action

Players use their voice to:

- Vote on new quest designs and seasonal themes

- Propose and approve Game rule changes

- Determine token distribution and reward structures

- Shape the Game's evolution and long-term vision

## Why Collective Governance Matters

### Building Trust Through Participation

When people have a voice in decisions that affect them, they become invested in outcomes. Collective governance transforms stakeholders into stewards.

### Distributed Wisdom

No single perspective holds all the answers. By bringing together domain experts, land projects, alliance organizations, investors, and eventually the people living in these communities, we access diverse wisdom and reduce blind spots.

### Regenerative Alignment

Governance structures that reflect regenerative principles - distributed, adaptive, inclusive, and growth-oriented - will naturally produce regenerative outcomes.

### Movement Infrastructure

ReGen Civics governance is more than just making decisions; it's about building the infrastructure for a regenerative movement. We're modeling what collective governance can look like at scale and exploring diverse strategies to achieve our goals.

---

## Governance as Movement Infrastructure

The governance systems we build today become the templates for tomorrow's regenerative societies. By creating transparent, participatory, and adaptive governance structures, we're not just managing a fund and a Game - we're demonstrating what's possible when we trust people to make wise decisions about their own futures.

The Regenerative Renaissance requires governance that evolves with our understanding, includes those most affected by decisions, and aligns incentives toward collective wellbeing. ReGen Civics governance is designed to do exactly that.
