### Healing the Five Bodies
**Season:** Spring | **Slug:** `healing-five-bodies`
**Rewards:** 144 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** Ongoing practice, at least one month

#### Story Card

Your body is not one thing. It is a nested system of systems, and ancient traditions across cultures have always known this. Different names in different lineages, but the same wisdom: you are not just a physical animal with a consciousness attached.

One way to understand this is through five bodies. The Physical: your actual flesh, bones, organs, the container that moves through space. The Emotional: the feeling life, the texture and temperature of what moves through you moment to moment. The Mental: the patterns of thought, belief, the stories you tell about who you are. The Spiritual: what reconnects you to something larger than yourself, the sacred. And at the deepest core, the Soul: the perfect and unchanging part of you that is simply witnessing all of this happen.

Most healing traditions agree on this basic structure, though they use different language. Some call them layers. Some call them bodies. What they all know is that health is not just about the physical body alone. You can have a fit body and a devastated emotional landscape. You can have a calm mind and a spirit that is starving.

This quest invites you to design a practice that tends to all five bodies, with attention to both the gathering and the releasing, the active and the receptive. We learn from masculine and feminine principles here not as gender, but as energies: the doing and the being, the illumination and the rest, the spring growth and the winter quieting. Each body needs both.

When you move through this quest, you are not just optimizing your health. You are learning to listen to yourself as a whole system, to recognize where you are imbalanced, and to respond with presence and intention. That self-knowledge becomes the foundation for everything else.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Study the five bodies.** Read or research the framework. Different traditions describe these differently. You might explore yoga's koshas, Celtic wisdom, or systems from your own heritage. Spend a week getting to know the concept. What does each body mean to you?

**Step 2: Assess your current state.** For each of the five bodies, write honestly about where you are. Physical: How is your energy, sleep, digestion, movement? Emotional: What do you feel regularly? What is stuck? Mental: What stories run on repeat? What beliefs do you carry? Spiritual: What practices connect you to something larger? Soul: Can you feel that still, unchanging presence within?

**Step 3: Design your practice.** Create a daily practice that tends to all five bodies. This might look like: morning movement (physical), journaling or feeling-work (emotional), meditation or study (mental), ceremony or nature time (spiritual), and a moment of stillness (soul). Include both active/gathering practices and receptive/releasing ones. The practice does not need to take hours. Fifteen to thirty minutes a day is enough if it is consistent.

**Step 4: Practice for one month.** Do your practice every day. Notice what shifts. What is easier? What is harder? Where do you feel the most resistance? Which body is calling for more attention?

**Step 5: Document and adjust.** Keep a simple record of what you did and what you noticed. After two weeks, adjust your practice based on what is working. After a month, write a reflection on what changed.

**Step 6: Create and share.** Write an article or make a short video (three minutes or less) describing your five-bodies practice. Include what you learned about yourself, which body was most challenging, which felt most rewarding. Post it publicly and submit the link.

#### Deliverable
A written reflection or video (three minutes or less) describing your five-bodies practice, the methods you used, and what you learned about yourself.

#### Tips
- Start simple. A practice does not need to be complicated to be powerful. Consistency matters more than complexity.
- Notice which bodies resist your attention. That is usually where the most growth happens.
- You can return to this quest each season. Your five-bodies practice will evolve as you do.
- If you are familiar with other frameworks (chakras, elements, systems), you can use those instead of the five bodies. The key is consistent, intentional care for all dimensions of yourself.
- Pay attention to both the practices that energize you (gathering) and the ones that help you release (letting go). You need both.

- The Body Keeps the Score by Bessel van der Kolk (understanding the physical and emotional body)
- Your own heritage tradition or wisdom lineage

### Study Natural Hygiene
**Season:** Spring | **Slug:** `study-natural-hygiene`
**Rewards:** 111 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One to two months

#### Story Card

A century ago, before pharmaceutical medicine became the default model of health in the West, there was another tradition. Natural Hygiene. The hygienists understood something that modern medicine has mostly forgotten: health is not something you get from someone else. Health is what your body naturally does when you stop poisoning it.

The framework is simple. Health comes from clean air, pure water, vital foods, rest, sunlight, and right relationship. Not from pills, not from procedures, not from fighting disease. Health is what happens when you stop doing harm and start creating the conditions for life to thrive.

The hygienists were not fringe. In the late 1800s and early 1900s, they ran sanitariums and healing centers across America. They worked with people dealing with what they called chronic disease, and they saw results that rivaled anything the pharmaceutical model could show. But pharmaceutical companies were richer and louder, and hygiene got quiet.

Now we are living in the results of that choice. Chronic disease is epidemic. People are sicker than they should be. And the pharmaceutical model does not seem to be working for most of it. Diabetes. Autoimmune. Neurological. Heart disease. These are not things you can pill away.

This quest invites you to study this old tradition seriously. To read the primary sources. To understand the principles. Then to actually live them for a full month and let your own body be the teacher. When you do, you will begin to understand something the hygienists knew: your body is not your enemy. Your body is trying to heal you. It has been trying this whole time. You just have to stop blocking it and give it what it actually needs.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Choose and read a primary text.** Read one full book from a Natural Hygiene author. The book should explain the foundational principles, not just recipes or tips. Spend two to three weeks on this.

**Step 2: Identify the core principles.** From your reading, write down the five to seven core principles of Natural Hygiene as you understand them. What are the most important ideas?

**Step 3: Commit to a full month of practice.** For the next thirty days, commit to living the principles as best you can. This means: pure water, vital foods (as close to what grows in your bioregion as possible), movement, sunlight, sleep, and clean air. Remove processed foods, alcohol, and other substances that degrade your system.

**Step 4: Document your observations.** Keep a simple log. What do you notice physically? Energy? Digestion? Sleep? Mental clarity? Mood? Physical pain or symptoms? Track what changes over the month.

**Step 5: Reflect on your understanding of your body.** After a month, write about what you have learned. Did your understanding of how your body works change? Did you notice things you had not noticed before? What surprised you?

**Step 6: Create and share.** Write an article or record a video sharing your key learnings. What was the most important principle you discovered? What practices are you keeping? What was hardest? What would you recommend to someone starting this journey? Post it and submit.

#### Deliverable
A written article or video (three to five minutes) sharing your experience and key learnings from studying and practicing Natural Hygiene.

#### Tips
- Primary texts are worth the effort. Rereading material you actually wrote in is different from just hearing about something.
- Your body's wisdom is more reliable than any theory. If a practice makes you feel better, it is working, regardless of whether you fully understand why.
- One month is a minimum. Most people need more time to feel the effects. If you are drawn to this work, consider extending the practice.
- Start where you are. If you cannot source entirely local food, do your best. The principles are about direction, not perfection.
- Withdrawal can happen when you remove substances your body is accustomed to. Mild headaches or fatigue are normal. This passes.

#### Resources
- Natural Hygiene: Man's Pristine Self by Shelton (foundational text, can be dense but authoritative)
- Mucusless Diet Healing System by Arnold Ehret (classic and readable)
- The Living on Light website and community
- Dr. Robert Morse teachings and videos

### ReGen Financial Systems: Launch a Community Currency
**Season:** Spring | **Slug:** `community-currency`
**Rewards:** 333 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** 2 to 3 months

#### Story Card

Imagine a currency designed by and for your community. Not one that flows out of the region constantly, extracting wealth. One that circulates locally, stays in the bioregion, strengthens the relationships between neighbors and local businesses. One that is governed by the people using it, not by a distant bank.

This is not imaginary. Communities around the world have done this. Bristol Pound in the UK. Sarafu in Kenya. SEEDS network across multiple countries. BerkShares in Massachusetts. Local currencies have different forms. Some are digital. Some are paper. Some start as time banks or skill-exchange systems. But they all follow the same principle: we decide what has value in our community, and we create a medium to exchange it.

The barriers to doing this are much lower than they used to be. Platforms like LocalScale and SEEDS have designed systems and tools (the Rainbow Seeds Protocol) specifically so communities can launch their own currencies without needing to be banking experts or blockchain developers. You coordinate with a small team, design your currency, invite vendors and community members, host a launch event, and begin circulating value through your own network.

This quest is not just about creating a currency. It is about building relationships between people and businesses in your bioregion. It is about learning how to design systems together. It is about creating the infrastructure for a regenerative economy that works for everyone in your place, not just those already wealthy.

When you complete this quest, you have more than a currency. You have a visible proof that another way is possible.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Research existing models.** Spend one week exploring at least three existing community currencies. Study how they were designed. Who governs them? How do they work practically? What was hardest about launching them? Make notes on what appeals to you and what seems hard.

**Step 2: Connect with LocalScale or SEEDS.** Reach out to either the LocalScale team or SEEDS foundation. Tell them you want to explore launching a community currency in your bioregion. They have done this many times and have resources, templates, and often people who can advise you through the process.

**Step 3: Form a core team.** Find two to five other people in your community who are genuinely interested in this work. This is not something you do alone. The team should include a mix of local business owners, community organizers, and regenerative agriculture folks if possible.

**Step 4: Design your currency.** Work with your team and the platform to design your currency. What do you call it? What are the principles behind it? How will it work technically? Who can use it? What is the initial distribution? This takes time and conversation. Plan for one to two months of meetings.

**Step 5: Recruit vendors and early users.** Begin reaching out to local businesses that you think would be interested. Restaurants, farms, repair shops, community services. Explain the vision. Invite them to be early adopters. Aim for at least twenty to thirty participating vendors by launch.

**Step 6: Host a launch event.** Create an event where you invite vendors, community members, and local leaders. Teach people about the currency. Get them to open accounts. Distribute the first units. Make it celebratory. This is a big deal.

**Step 7: Document and share.** Throughout the process, document. Take photos and video of your core team meetings, the recruitment process, vendor conversations, the launch event. Write about the design choices you made and why. Share your learnings openly.

#### Deliverable
A comprehensive case study (article or short video series) documenting your design process, your vendor recruitment, your launch event, and the results. Include reflection on challenges, what worked, and what you would do differently.

#### Tips
- Start with vendors and businesses you already have relationships with. They are more likely to take a risk with you.
- Education is everything. Most people have never heard of community currencies. You will spend a lot of time explaining. That is okay.
- The launch event is the moment when the currency becomes real to people. Make it good. Music, food, celebration. People need to feel like they are joining something alive.
- You do not need to have it perfectly designed before you start. You can launch and adjust. Iteration is normal.
- Reach out to land projects, farmers markets, and local food networks first. They often align with regenerative economics already.
- The governance structure matters. Think early about how decisions will be made and who gets a say.

- SEEDS Foundation (https://www.joinseeds.earth/)
- Rainbow Seeds Protocol documentation
- Sarafu Network (case study in digital community currency)
- Bristol Pound (case study in paper community currency)

### Friendship with a Free Animal
**Season:** Summer | **Slug:** `friendship-free-animal`
**Rewards:** 111 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** Ongoing, minimum one season

#### Story Card

There is a difference between taming an animal and befriending one. Taming is about control. You feed the animal until it becomes dependent on you. It stays because it needs you. That is not friendship.

Real friendship is built on mutual choice. The animal chooses to be in relationship with you. You choose to be in relationship with the animal. Both of you keep your autonomy. Both of you choose to show up.

This is possible with wild and semi-wild animals. Deer. Birds. Foxes. Rabbits. Squirrels. Animals that are not domesticated but are not entirely wary of humans. The work is patient. It is about learning a language that is not words.

That language exists. Interspecies telepathy is not magic. It is a capacity all humans have and have used for millennia. Most of us just lost the practice. To communicate with an animal, you quiet your mind. You become present. You visualize a clear image of what you want to convey. You feel the emotion behind the message. You send it toward the animal with intention. Then you wait and listen. Not with your ears. With your attention.

Many humans report that when they do this genuinely, animals respond. They come closer. They make eye contact. They change their behavior in ways that show they understood. Some researchers call this animal telepathy. Some call it reading subtle body language and energetic shifts. The mechanism does not matter. What matters is it works.

When you do this practice seriously over a season or more, something shifts inside you. You stop feeling separate from the other animals. You begin to see the intelligence in them, the presence, the consciousness. You realize you have been living in a world full of other persons, and you just did not know how to listen.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Choose your animal.** Find a location where a specific wild or semi-wild animal regularly appears. A park bench where ravens come. A garden where deer browse. A burrow where a fox lives. Somewhere you can return to consistently over months, and the animal will begin to recognize you.

**Step 2: Spend time being present.** For the first month, just be there. Sit quietly. Watch. Do not try to interact. Do not make sudden movements. Let the animal become accustomed to your presence. Bring the same energy each time you go. Calm. Present. Unhurried.

**Step 3: Learn the animal's patterns.** Notice when it appears. What it eats. Who it travels with. Its habits and preferences. What makes it nervous. What makes it curious. Become a student of that being.

**Step 4: Begin gentle offering.** Once the animal seems comfortable with your presence, you can offer something. Food is the easiest. But offer without attachment. Put it down and step back. Let the animal choose whether to take it. If it does, do not react with excitement. Remain calm. This is the difference between feeding to create dependency and offering respect.

**Step 5: Practice interspecies telepathy.** Once you feel a baseline of trust, try communicating silently. Quiet your mind completely. Form a clear visual image of something simple you want to convey (I am here. I see you. I come in peace). Feel the emotion behind it. Direct the thought toward the animal with intention. Then be silent. Wait. What shifts? Does the animal move closer? Change its behavior? Make eye contact? The response may be subtle.

**Step 6: Develop the relationship.** Over weeks and months, return regularly. The relationship deepens through consistency and presence. Some animals will begin seeking you out. Some will allow you to get closer. Some will make vocalizations. Each animal is different.

**Step 7: Document the journey.** Take photos and notes throughout the process. Not for proof, but for your own reflection. Write about what you notice about the animal's personality, intelligence, and presence. How does your relationship to nature shift as you develop this friendship?

**Step 8: Share your experience.** Create a photo essay or short video series following the development of your friendship. Write or speak about what you learned about attention, patience, non-human intelligence, and what it means to be in genuine relationship with another being.

#### Deliverable
A photo essay, short video series, or written narrative documenting the development of your friendship with the animal over at least one season. Include reflection on what you learned about attention, patience, and the intelligence of the other being.

#### Tips
- Consistency is everything. Show up at the same time, the same place, regularly. Animals remember.
- Stay calm and unhurried. Animals read your energy. If you are excited or needy, they will feel it and may withdraw.
- Food as offering, not as control. Once you resort to dependency feeding to make an animal stay with you, the friendship is gone.
- Telepathy works best when you have genuinely quiet your mind. Meditation or breathwork before you practice helps.
- Different animals have different personalities. Some are playful. Some are cautious. Some are bold. Learn to recognize and respect the temperament of your specific friend.
- Safety first. You are not domesticating the animal. Respect its wildness. Do not push for contact. Let contact happen on the animal's terms.
- This quest teaches patience in a world addicted to speed. That is part of the value.

- Local naturalists or wildlife educators in your area
- Deer or bird identification guides for your bioregion

### Your Honey Moon: Fasting with Honey and Bee Pollen
**Season:** Summer | **Slug:** `honey-moon`
**Rewards:** 144 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One lunar cycle (28 to 29 days)

#### Story Card

Honey is a complete food. It contains vitamins, minerals, amino acids, enzymes, and antioxidants. Bee pollen is nearly as complete. Together, they cover nearly everything your body needs nutritionally. This is not poetic metaphor. This is measurable biochemistry.

Thousands of years ago, humans understood this. In many cultures, honey and bee pollen were used as a fasting medicine. Couples about to marry would undergo a honey moon: thirty days of eating primarily honey and bee pollen, supplemented with water and herbal teas. The purpose was purification and preparation.

For males, the practice was understood to increase fertility and sperm health. For females, it increased reproductive health and hormonal balance. Both sexes would emerge from the month clearer, lighter, and more vital. That clarity and vitality was understood to be the right container for beginning new life together.

The science supports this. Honey contains B vitamins that support nervous system function and mood. Bee pollen contains nearly complete amino acid profiles. Fasting on these foods gives your digestive system a rest while still providing complete nutrition. After days of this, most people report increased mental clarity, improved sleep, and a sense of lightness that is not deprivation. It is the opposite: abundance in simplicity.

This quest is not about becoming thin. It is about clearing. When you fast for a month on honey and bee pollen, your body is not starving. It is cleaning. Old residues move out. Inflammation settles. Your nervous system begins to find equilibrium. You experience what happens when you stop constantly processing complex foods and let your system recalibrate.

The honey moon is an ancient initiatory practice. In doing it, you are invoking something primal and wise that humans have always known.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Gather high-quality ingredients.** Source raw honey and bee pollen from a local beekeeper or trusted supplier if possible. Raw, unheated, unfiltered honey is vastly superior to processed versions. Bee pollen should be fresh. Plan for about one to two cups of honey per day and one to three tablespoons of pollen daily. Get a variety of herbal teas you enjoy.

**Step 2: Prepare physically and mentally.** Spend a few days before beginning to reduce processed foods, caffeine, and alcohol. Tell people close to you what you are doing. They will notice. A gentle fast is not comfortable for everyone around you if they do not understand it. Mental preparation matters as much as physical.

**Step 3: Start during the new moon if possible.** If you can align your fast with a lunar cycle, do. Begin on the new moon. Observe the lunar phases as you move through your month. The moon is tied to cycles and rhythm in your body.

**Step 4: Eat honey and bee pollen as your primary foods.** For the full twenty-eight to twenty-nine days, eat primarily raw honey and bee pollen. You can mix them with water to make a tea. You can eat them straight. You can mix them with herbal infusions. Drink plenty of pure water. Herbal teas (no caffeine) as desired. If you feel genuinely dizzy or unwell, you can add a small amount of fruit or a simple grain, but try to stay with the mono-diet as much as possible.

**Step 5: Track your experience daily.** Each day, write a few notes. Energy level? Sleep quality? Mental clarity? Mood? Physical sensations? What shifts? Most people notice significant changes by day five to seven.

**Step 6: Notice the clarity emerging.** By the second week, many people experience marked improvement in mood, clarity, and energy. Some report visual clarity. Some report emotional shifts. Some simply report feeling lighter. Observe without judgment.

**Step 7: Complete the full cycle.** When you reach day twenty-eight or twenty-nine, do not stop abruptly. Spend the next few days slowly reintroducing other foods. Simple foods first. Fruit. Vegetables. Grains. Build complexity gradually over a week.

**Step 8: Reflect and share.** After you have completed and reintegrated, write about the experience. What changed in your body? Your mind? Your mood? What surprised you? Would you do it again? Create a post or video for the community.

#### Deliverable
A written reflection or short video (three to five minutes) describing your experience of the honey moon. Include what you noticed in your body, mind, and overall clarity. Share any insights about food, fasting, or your relationship to eating.

#### Tips
- The first three to five days can feel challenging as your body adjusts. This passes. Stay hydrated and trust the process.
- Energy levels may dip slightly in the first week, then improve significantly. This is normal.
- You might notice old symptoms resurfacing briefly as your body detoxifies. This is also normal and temporary.
- If you have a history of eating disorders, this quest may not be right for you. Consult with a healer you trust.
- The honey and pollen should taste good to you. If they do not, you will not stay committed. Find sources you genuinely enjoy.
- Herbal support can help. Nettle tea for minerals. Ginger for digestion. Tulsi for calm. Experiment with what your body asks for.
- Some people do the honey moon at other times of year. If summer does not work, adapt. The practice is what matters.

### Singing to Your Food Forest
**Season:** Summer | **Slug:** `singing-food-forest`
**Rewards:** 77 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** Daily practice, one month

#### Story Card

Plants respond to sound. This is not speculation. It is measurable. Different frequencies of sound affect plant growth, flowering, and fruiting. Some frequencies increase yield. Some create stress. The mechanisms are not entirely understood, but the effect is documented.

But that is not why we sing to plants.

We sing to plants because it deepens our relationship to them. When you sing to something you tend, you cannot help but slow down. You have to show up consistently. You have to be present. You have to pay attention to the being you are singing to. Over time, that plant becomes less abstract and more alive to you. It becomes a relationship, not an object.

This is the beginning of regenerative practice. When you see the land as a collection of objects to extract value from, you behave one way. When you see it as a community of living beings you are in relationship with, you behave differently. You take care. You listen. You respond. Singing is one of the oldest ways to move from the first orientation to the second.

You do not need to be a musician. You do not need to write beautiful songs. Some of the best singing to plants is simple. Humming while you water. Wordless sounds made up on the spot. Songs you have known since childhood. What matters is the presence and the intention.

When you sing, you are saying to the plant: you matter to me. You are alive. I see you. I am in relationship with you. Over a month of this practice, that message sinks into both the plant and into you. The boundary between you and the garden begins to soften.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Choose what to sing to.** Pick a specific garden, food forest, or patch of land that you will visit regularly. This could be your own garden, a community garden, a wild area near your home. Choose a place you genuinely love.

**Step 2: Create a daily singing practice.** Each day for thirty days, spend at least fifteen to twenty minutes singing to the plants. You can sing the same song every time or create new ones spontaneously. Use your voice, an instrument, or both. Let it be simple.

**Step 3: Try different approaches.** Sing words about the plant, its health, and vitality. Sing wordless tones. Use call-and-response: you sing a phrase, then imagine the plant singing back. Hum while you water. Let the music be playful.

**Step 4: Pay attention to the plants.** As you sing, notice details about each plant. The color of the leaves. The shape of the stem. The number of flowers or buds. Are there signs of health? Signs of stress? Insects? These observations deepen the relationship.

**Step 5: Document the practice.** Take photos of the garden or forest at the beginning, middle, and end of the month. Keep a simple log: what you sang, how long, what you noticed in the plants. This becomes your record of the practice.

**Step 6: Notice what shifts inside you.** This is as much about your inner transformation as it is about the plants. What happens to your sense of hurry? Your sense of separation from nature? Your stress levels? Your joy? These inner shifts are part of the quest.

**Step 7: Share your practice.** Create a short video or photo essay showing your garden or forest and your singing practice. Talk about what changed in how you relate to the land. Include a few seconds of you singing if you feel comfortable. Post it for the community.

#### Deliverable
A photo essay or short video (two to three minutes) documenting your month of singing to your food forest. Include a sample of the singing and reflection on what changed in your relationship to the plants and land.

#### Tips
- You do not need to be a good singer. Truly bad singing to plants is completely fine. The plants do not care about technique.
- Consistency matters more than length. Fifteen minutes every day is better than two hours once a week.
- Singing first thing in the morning or at sunset can make the practice feel more ritualistic and powerful.
- If you feel self-conscious singing out loud, start with humming. As you get more comfortable, your voice will expand.
- Different songs for different plants can be fun. Notice which plants seem to respond to which sounds.
- You might discover that the plants are teaching you what they need through sound. Stay open to that.
- This practice often leads to noticing other things: insects, soil quality, water needs. Follow those observations.

- Singing to Plants by various herbalists and musicians
- Simple songwriting resources (no music experience needed)
- Your own intuition and voice

### Animal Spirit Totems and Bioregional Clan
**Season:** Summer | **Slug:** `animal-spirit-totems`
**Rewards:** 111 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** 2 to 4 weeks of research and creation

#### Story Card

Every place has a spirit. Every bioregion has animals and plants and elements that are its core beings. These are the ones that shaped the landscape. The ones humans have always lived in relationship with. The ones that teach.

Many cultures have ways of identifying these core beings and creating a clan identity around them. A group of people in a specific place might identify as Wolf Clan, or Cedar People, or the ones who carry the energy of the river running through the bioregion. This is not about costume or fantasy role-play. It is about identity rooted in place.

When you create a bioregional clan identity, you are doing something ancient. You are saying: we belong here. We are part of this specific place. These are the beings that taught us. We carry their gifts. This is our home.

The first step is understanding your bioregion deeply. What animals are most significant? Predators and prey? Pollinators? The ones that have the most presence? What plants have shaped the landscape? What elements dominate: mountain, river, forest, desert? What fungi hold the soil together? What insects are the foundation of everything else?

Then you listen to your own resonance. Which of these beings do you feel called to? Not which sounds cool. Which one, when you think about it, feels like home? Which one contains lessons you need? Which one, when you close your eyes, feels like a part of you?

Once you have identified these, you create something. Art. A costume or ceremonial dress. A song. A story. Writing. Movement. Something that expresses the relationship and makes it visible. This is not decoration. This is an act of commitment.

Finally, you find others in your bioregion doing similar work and you weave those identities into a collective clan. You become the Wolf People of this valley. The Cedar Ones of this watershed. A visible, named group with a shared commitment to the place you live.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1: Research your bioregion deeply.** Spend at least one week learning everything you can about the non-human beings significant to your place. What are the apex predators? The keystone species? The most abundant plants? The important fungi? What cycles dominate the year? Talk to locals who have lived there a long time. Read natural history. Spend time outside observing.

**Step 2: Document the most significant beings.** Create a list of at least five to ten non-human beings most central to your bioregion. For each one, write what you know: behavior, ecological role, cultural significance to indigenous or historical peoples of the place.

**Step 3: Identify your personal totems.** From this list, which ones call to you? Not for surface reasons, but for what they represent or teach. Which do you feel a kinship with? Identify three to five personal totems. These are beings whose energy you want to carry.

**Step 4: Research the traditions.** If your bioregion has indigenous peoples or long-standing cultural traditions, research how they would identify and honor animal totems. If you have your own ancestral wisdom traditions, explore those. The method matters less than the sincerity.

**Step 5: Create your expression.** Make something that embodies your connection to these totems. This could be:
   - Visual art (painting, sculpture, collage)
   - A costume or piece of ceremonial clothing
   - A song or musical piece
   - Written poetry or prose
   - A dance or movement piece
   - A multimedia creation combining multiple forms

The creation should feel like a genuine expression of your relationship, not a performance.

**Step 6: Gather others.** Reach out to people in your bioregion who might be interested in this work. Other questers, land regenerators, community members who love the place. Invite them to create their own totem expressions and then gather together to share and weave a collective clan identity.

**Step 7: Name your clan and create a commitment.** As a group, identify what unites you. What are the shared totems or bioregional beings that represent your collective? Name your clan (e.g., "The River People," "Hawk Collective," "The Ones Who Tend the Chestnuts"). Create a simple statement of what it means to be part of this clan.

**Step 8: Document and share.** Create a photo essay or video showing the individual totem creations and the process of gathering as a clan. Write about what you learned about your bioregion and what it means to claim a place-based identity. Share the collective clan statement.

#### Deliverable
A comprehensive documentation including:
1. Your personal totem creation (photo or video of the piece itself)
2. A written reflection on your research process and which bioregional beings called to you
3. Documentation of your clan gathering (photos, video) and the clan identity you created
4. A written statement of what it means to be part of your bioregional clan
5. The names and basic information about the clan members

#### Tips
- If you feel disconnected from your bioregion, this quest is an invitation to deepen that connection. Start with research. Let love grow from knowledge.
- Indigenous peoples of your region can be teachers if you approach respectfully. Many tribes offer education and you can support their work directly.
- Your totem does not have to be glamorous. A toad or a beetle or a fungus can be as powerful as a wolf or eagle.
- The creation does not need to be artistic in a formal sense. Authentic feeling matters more than technical skill.
- Clan is collective. This part takes time and community. You cannot do this alone. Be patient in finding your people.
- Some clans might work toward shared land projects or conservation efforts. This can deepen the identity over time.
- Document the process, not just the end result. Show the research, the conversations, the gathering.
