### Future Casting
**Season:** Fall | **Slug:** `future-casting`
**Rewards:** 77 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One journey session, plus writing time (2-4 hours)

#### Story Card

Close your eyes and travel forward. Not in hope or wishfulness, but in actual sensory experience. You move through time to a day in a thriving regenerative future. What does the air smell like? What textures meet your hands? What tastes are on your tongue? You watch people living. You see what they build, how they move, what they've stopped doing and started doing instead.

This is not speculation. It's memory from a future that's already real in some dimension, and you're just visiting. When you return, you write what you experienced. Not a wish list of what you hope for. A specific, grounded, sensory account of one real day you actually traveled to.

Then you share it. As you do, everyone else shares theirs. Together, the community builds a living collective vision of what we're moving toward. That vision becomes real first in our bodies and imaginations. Then it becomes real in the world.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Set a time and place where you won't be interrupted. 45 minutes minimum.

**Step 2:** Choose your method. Guided meditation, breathwork, journeying music, or silent sitting. Whatever gets you into a state where you can genuinely travel.

**Step 3:** Travel forward in time. Move to a future where regeneration has taken root. Step into a specific day. Experience it fully with all your senses.

**Step 4:** Note what you see, hear, smell, taste, touch. Notice what people are doing. Notice what has changed. Stay until the vision fades naturally.

**Step 5:** Return to this time and place. Ground yourself. Drink water.

**Step 6:** Write your account. Not filtered, not polished. Raw and real. What did you actually experience?

**Step 7:** Post your story to the forum. Read what others experienced. Notice what repeats across the visions. Notice what's unique to each person.

#### Deliverable
A written account (300-800 words) of a specific day in the regenerative future you visited. Posted to the forum where others can read and comment.

#### Tips
- Don't pre-plan what you'll see. Let it come to you.
- If nothing comes, that's information too. Write about that.
- The more senses you engage, the more real it becomes.
- Write immediately after you return while the memory is fresh.

### Eating Sunlight
**Season:** Fall | **Slug:** `eating-sunlight`
**Rewards:** 99 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One month daily practice

#### Story Card

Food that's alive tastes different. It feels different in your body. Your system recognizes it as food that just arrived, still warm from photosynthesis, not something that's been sitting in storage for weeks.

This quest is about that recognition. For thirty days, you eat as much as you can directly from living plants. Leaves from your garden. Berries from the wild. Herbs you find. Greens that just came out of the ground. You're training your body and senses to remember what real food feels like.

The industrial food system depends on you forgetting this. Depends on you eating things that died weeks ago, traveled thousands of miles, lost most of their nutrition and aliveness in the process. When you spend a month eating alive food, your body wakes up. You start to notice the difference. You start to crave real food. And that craving changes how you move in the world.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Plan your food sources. Map where you can forage. Find gardens to visit. Know what's safe to eat and what's toxic in your area.

**Step 2:** Start a daily practice. Every day, eat at least one meal or snack directly from a living plant with minimal processing.

**Step 3:** Track what you notice. How do you feel? What changes in your energy, digestion, mood, clarity?

**Step 4:** Pay attention to cravings. What your body wants after a week of alive food is different from what it wanted before.

**Step 5:** Experiment with variety. Different leaves, berries, herbs. Notice how each one tastes and feels in your body.

**Step 6:** Document your observations. Keep notes on energy, digestion, mood, cravings, surprises.

**Step 7:** At the end of the month, write or record your reflection. What changed? What do you notice now that you've paused processed food?

#### Deliverable
A reflection piece (400-700 words) or 10-minute video/audio documenting what you noticed over the month. Include at least three specific observations about your body, energy, or cravings. Post to the forum.

#### Tips
- Start by identifying 3-5 food sources near you that you can visit regularly.
- Raw leafy greens, berries, and certain herbs are safest if you're new to foraging.
- Wash everything. Use local foraging guides to be certain of identification.
- Expect the first week to be adjustment. By week three, the shift becomes clear.

#### Resources
- Local foraging guide for your region
- Wild edibles identification app or book
- Community garden or u-pick locations near you
- Forum thread: #Fall-Quests-2026

#### Connected To
- Future Casting (both are about direct sensory experience)
- Decrease Expenses (eating alive food costs less than processed food)

### Becoming Trauma Informed
**Season:** Fall | **Slug:** `becoming-trauma-informed`
**Rewards:** 111 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One week of watching, ongoing integration

#### Story Card

Trauma is not an exception. It's the baseline for our culture. Most of us carry it, and most of us don't know we're carrying it. We mistake it for normal. We build systems that perpetuate it. We pass it to our children.

When you understand trauma, everything shifts. You see behavior differently. You see addiction differently. You see conflict and why it repeats. You see your own patterns and your family patterns with clearer eyes. You see what healing actually requires.

This quest is about watching Gabor Mate's masterclass and letting that understanding sink into your bones. Then you apply it. To yourself. To your family history. To the communities you're part of. You write what you see. You share it. You start healing.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Watch Gabor Mate's Trauma Masterclass in full. It's available on YouTube. Block out time. Don't half-watch it while doing other things.

**Step 2:** Take notes as you watch. What lands? What surprises you? What makes you uncomfortable?

**Step 3:** Reflect on your own life. Where do you see trauma responses in yourself? In your patterns? In your family history?

**Step 4:** Look at the communities you're part of. How does unprocessed trauma show up in group dynamics? In conflict? In how decisions get made?

**Step 5:** Consider what healing would look like for you personally. What would it take?

**Step 6:** Write a reflection. This is not just intellectual. What did you learn about yourself?

**Step 7:** Share your reflection in the forum. Engage with others who've done the same quest.

#### Deliverable
A written reflection (500-1000 words) or 15-minute video/audio that includes at least three insights from the masterclass and one personal application. How does understanding trauma change your understanding of yourself or your communities?

#### Tips
- Don't try to fix anyone while doing this quest. Start with understanding yourself.
- Trauma responses are smart. They protected you. Honor that even as you work to transform them.
- If this work brings up deep stuff, find a therapist or somatic practitioner. Do not do this alone if you're in crisis.
- Watch the masterclass more than once if you need to. Let it sink in slowly.

**Season:** Winter | **Slug:** `write-childrens-book`
**Rewards:** 177 $ReGen + 1 RVoice

#### Story Card

Imagine a child waking up in a regenerative world. What does she see from her window? What grows around her home? Who comes to visit her family? How does she spend her day? What does she learn? Who are her teachers?

This is not a book about problems fixed. It's a book about a world that works. About a day in a life where food grows everywhere, where communities are built around human and ecological flourishing, where children and elders spend time together, where the water is clean and the soil is alive.

Write it true to that world. Make it specific. Make it real enough that a child reading it can actually imagine living there. Illustrate it if you can. Share it with the movement. Let it be a seed of what we're building toward.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Clarify your vision. What age group are you writing for? What is one day in the life of a child in your regenerative world?

**Step 2:** Outline the story. Five to ten key moments in that day. What does the child see, do, learn, feel?

**Step 4:** Illustrate if you can. Drawings, collage, photos, AI-generated images. Make the world visible.

**Step 5:** Have someone outside your head read it. Does the world feel real? Does a child want to live there?

**Step 6:** Refine based on feedback.

**Step 7:** Share your finished book with the community. Post to the forum with illustrations and text.

A complete illustrated children's book (500-2000 words with images) depicting a realistic day in a child's life in a regenerative world. Posted to the forum where the community can read and respond.

- Avoid preachy or corrective language. Show the world, don't explain why the old world was bad.

### Recreate Your Personal Cycles
**Season:** Winter | **Slug:** `recreate-personal-cycles`
**Rewards:** 111 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One month of tracking, one month of practice

#### Story Card

The industrial work week doesn't match any natural cycle. Five days on, two days off. Same hours regardless of season. No respect for your body's actual rhythms. No alignment with moon or sun.

Your body knows natural cycles. The moon pulls the tides and your body is mostly water. The seasons shift your energy. Your circadian rhythm wants to follow the sun. Your creative and social needs pulse in patterns that don't match a spreadsheet.

This quest is about mapping those patterns and rebuilding your personal calendar around them. Not fighting your nature. Working with it. You track yourself across at least one lunar cycle. You notice when you have energy, when you need rest, when you want to be social, when you need solitude, when creativity flows and when it stalls. Then you design a personal calendar that honors those rhythms.

You live that calendar for a month. You document what changes.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Learn about natural cycles. Study lunar phases, solar seasons, circadian rhythms, and the thirteen-moon calendar. Understand how they work.

**Step 2:** Track yourself for one full lunar cycle (28-30 days). Record your energy, creativity, social needs, focus, rest needs, mood, and physical state each day.

**Step 3:** Look for patterns. When does your energy peak? When do you crash? When do you want connection? When do you need solitude? When are you most creative?

**Step 4:** Redesign your personal calendar. Allocate deep work during your focus peaks. Plan social activities when you want connection. Schedule rest days when your body asks for them. Align your weeks with lunar phases if that calls to you.

**Step 5:** Live your new calendar for one full month. Actually do it. Don't just plan it.

**Step 6:** Document what changes. How does it feel to honor your actual rhythms instead of fighting them?

**Step 7:** Share your system and observations with the community. What did you learn? How did your wellbeing shift?

#### Deliverable
A written or visual documentation (500-1000 words or video) that includes your tracking data, the patterns you discovered, your new personal calendar, and observations from living it for a month. Post to the forum.

#### Tips
- Download a calendar app that lets you track multiple categories. Or use a simple spreadsheet.
- Track at least five categories: energy level, focus capacity, social desire, creative flow, and rest need.
- Your patterns may not match anyone else's. Honor your actual rhythm, not what you think you should be.
- This usually gets easier after the first month. Stick with it through at least two months to feel the real benefit.

#### Resources
- Lunar calendar for your hemisphere
- "The Fifth Season" by N.K. Jemisin (explores cycles and time)
- Cycle tracking app: Clue, Eve, or similar
- Danur Khn's work on circadian rhythm
- Forum thread: #Winter-Quests-2026

#### Connected To
- Make a Song (rhythm and cycles are foundational to music)
- Recreating personal cycles aligns with Ringing Cedars philosophy of living in natural harmony

### Decrease Expenses, Increase Joy
**Season:** Anytime | **Slug:** `decrease-expenses`
**Rewards:** 77 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** One month of tracking

#### Story Card

We're taught that the answer to not having enough is to make more money. Earn more. Buy more. Own more. But there's another path. What if you spent less and felt better? What if the things you cut weren't actually making you happy anyway?

This quest asks you to look at where your money goes. Really look. You track everything for a month. Then you identify the purchases that didn't actually increase your joy or wellbeing. The subscriptions you forgot about. The impulse buys that felt good for five minutes. The things you bought because you thought you should.

Then you replace at least three of them with alternatives that cost less and feel better. Maybe it's free time instead of paid entertainment. Community instead of purchased goods. Making something instead of buying it.

You document the before and after. What did you cut? What did you replace it with? Did it feel like a loss or a gain? This shift reduces what the system says you need. And reducing what you need increases your freedom.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Track every dollar you spend for thirty days. Be honest. Include everything.

**Step 2:** At the end of the month, review your spending. Group it by category.

**Step 3:** Identify purchases that did not increase your joy or wellbeing. Which ones made you feel less good afterward than before?

**Step 4:** Pick at least three to eliminate or replace.

**Step 5:** Find alternatives. Free or low-cost options that actually feel better. Community gathering instead of paid event. Homemade meal instead of restaurant. Library instead of bookstore.

**Step 6:** Live the new pattern for at least two weeks. Notice how it feels.

**Step 7:** Create a before-and-after reflection. Video, article, or voice recording. What changed? How does your life feel?

#### Deliverable
A video (3-5 minutes), article (400-600 words), or audio recording (5-10 minutes) documenting three specific spending changes and how they impacted your life. Include honest reflection on whether it felt like a gain or sacrifice.

#### Tips
- The goal is not deprivation. It's alignment. Cut things that don't actually serve you.
- Community and time often feel better than purchased goods. Notice that.
- Some spending cuts reveal habits you didn't realize you had. That's the point.
- Small changes compound. Three replacements create momentum toward bigger shifts.

#### Resources
- Spending tracker app: Mint, YNAB, or simple spreadsheet
- Community resources in your area: free libraries, community gardens, skill-share groups
- "Your Money or Your Life" by Vicki Robin and Joe Dominguez (optional reading)
- Forum thread: #Anytime-Quests with spending-shift stories

### Start a Friend Pool
**Season:** Anytime | **Slug:** `start-friend-pool`
**Rewards:** 111 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** Ongoing, start small

#### Story Card

Sharing resources is not a sacrifice. It's smarter. Three to ten people pooling what they have can meet everyone's needs with less individual strain. A shared tool library means no one needs to own a drill. A shared grocery run means less trips, lower costs, and time together. A shared savings fund means you can do things no one could afford alone.

The best place to practice this is with friends you already trust. Where the stakes are low enough to learn, where failure doesn't destroy relationships, where you can actually talk about what works and what doesn't.

This quest is about building one small pool and documenting how you do it. What agreements did you make? What did you learn? What worked? What was harder than you expected?

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Gather your group. Three to ten friends who are interested in resource sharing.

**Step 2:** Decide what you're pooling. Tools, groceries, money, skills, land access, childcare. Start with something specific and manageable.

**Step 3:** Make your agreements explicit. Who contributes what? How do decisions get made? What happens if someone wants to leave? How do you handle disagreements?

**Step 4:** Document your agreements. In writing. Shared with everyone.

**Step 5:** Start small and live it for at least two months.

**Step 6:** Check in together regularly. What's working? What's not? What do you want to adjust?

**Step 7:** Document your learning. What did you discover about collective resource management? What surprised you? What would you do differently next time?

#### Deliverable
A written guide (600-1000 words) or video (10-15 minutes) documenting your pool structure, agreements, learnings, and recommendations for others starting a similar group. Include honest reflection on challenges and successes.

#### Tips
- Keep it simple at first. One tool library or one monthly grocery run, not five things at once.
- Written agreements matter. Verbal agreements fall apart quickly.
- Regular check-ins prevent resentment from building.
- Start with people you actually like and trust. That foundation matters.

#### Resources
- Cooperative economics resources (ICA, Cooperation Jackson)
- Tool library models (check if one exists in your area)
- Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) for grocery models
- Forum thread: #Anytime-Quests with pool examples and templates

### Present Parenting: The First Three Years
**Season:** Anytime | **Slug:** `present-parenting`
**Rewards:** 444 $ReGen + 1 RVoice
**Time:** Three years (ongoing commitment)

#### Story Card

This is a hero quest. Not because it's easy. Because it's the most important work some of us will ever do.

The first three years of a child's life are when the subconscious mind develops. Some researchers argue these are the most important years of our entire lives. And yet our culture pushes us to return to productivity as fast as possible. To extract ourselves from presence. To compartmentalize childcare.

This quest is an invitation to something different. Whatever it takes, you give your child at least eight hours per day of genuine attentive presence. Reduced work, radical simplification, community support, co-parenting arrangements, whatever it takes.

You document the practice. The challenges. The support systems you built. What you observed in your child and yourself. Because this is hard. It brings up your own traumas from that age. And doing it with care, attention and presence is the most powerful gift you could give. To your child. To yourself.

#### How To Do This Quest

**Step 1:** Commit to the practice. Eight hours minimum of attentive presence each day for three years.

**Step 2:** Restructure your life around this. Work less. Simplify. Build community support. Find co-parenting arrangements. Do what's necessary.

**Step 3:** Identify what presence actually means for you. Not hovering. Not entertainment. Attentive, responsive, engaged.

**Step 4:** As you practice, document regularly. Monthly check-ins at minimum. What are you noticing? What's changing?

**Step 5:** Expect your own stuff to come up. Your own traumas. Your own unmet needs from that age. Consider therapy or somatic work alongside this practice.

**Step 6:** Build community. You cannot do this alone. Find other parents, grandparents, elders. Share the work and the burden.

**Step 7:** After three years, write your full account. What did you observe? How did your child develop? How did you change? What would you do differently?

#### Deliverable
A comprehensive account (1500-3000 words) or video series (30-60 minutes total) documenting your three years of present parenting. Include challenges faced, support systems built, observations about your child's development, your own transformation, and recommendations for others considering this path.

#### Tips
- Community is essential. Find other parents doing similar work. You need their support.
- Your child's behavior is always communication. Presence lets you understand what they're saying.
- Your own healing matters as much as your child's. Do that work alongside this.
- Some days you'll fail. You'll lose presence. That's normal. Start again tomorrow.

#### Resources
- "The Explosive Child" by Ross Greene
- "How to Talk So Kids Will Listen" by Adele Faber and Elaine Mazlish
- "Attached" by Amir Levine and Rachel Heller
- Somatic practitioners and therapists who understand early childhood
- Forum thread: #Anytime-Quests for present-parenting community
