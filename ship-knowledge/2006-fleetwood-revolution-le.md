# The Ship: 2006 Fleetwood Revolution LE knowledge pack

Seed knowledge for the Shipwright. Each `##` section becomes one knowledge chunk.
Header format: `## Title | system: <system> | source: <where this came from> | type: manual|service_bulletin|forum_wisdom`

Facts below come from the official 2006 Revolution LE brochure (Fleetwood REVLE06F2) unless the
source says otherwise. Where floorplans differ (40E / 40J / 40L) the chunk says so. Verify anything
critical against her own data plates and manuals; brochure numbers are approximate by Fleetwood's
own disclaimer.

## Engine and drivetrain basics | system: engine | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

She runs a Caterpillar C9 diesel, 8.8 liters, 400 HP, 1150 lb-ft of torque at 1400 RPM, with a
side-mounted radiator. Transmission is an Allison 3000 MH 6-speed automatic with electronic
shifter. Alternator is a 160 amp Leece Neville. Fuel tank holds 125 gallons of diesel. She has an
exhaust brake; use it on long downgrades instead of riding the service brakes.

## Chassis, suspension, and weights | system: chassis | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Spartan modular chassis (Fleetwood calls it the Liberty Chassis) with I-beam suspension, Bilstein
shocks, and air suspension with a manual air dump switch on the dash. Weights: GVWR 34,000 lbs,
front axle rating 14,000 lbs, rear axle rating 20,000 lbs, GCWR 49,000 lbs. Hitch receiver is rated
15,000 lbs with 1,500 lbs tongue weight, and Spartan recommends a supplemental brake system on
anything towed. Wheelbase is 278 inches on the 40E floorplan, 266 inches on 40J and 40L.

## Air brakes: what a driver must know | system: tires_brakes | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure + standard air brake practice | type: manual

She has full air brakes with automatic slack adjusters and ABS. Air brakes need pressure before
they release: after starting, let the compressor build until the gauges read normal operating
pressure (roughly 100 to 125 psi) and the low-air warning stops before moving. If air pressure is
lost while driving, the spring brakes will drag and then lock; pull over safely and stop. Never
drive with a low-air warning active. A morning walk-around should include listening for air leaks
after the system is charged.

## Tires and wheels | system: tires_brakes | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Goodyear 295/70R 22.5 H tires on aluminum wheels. Correct cold pressure depends on actual axle
weight; weigh her loaded and use the Goodyear load-inflation table rather than the sidewall max.
A tire pressure check gauge came with the coach. Check pressures cold before each travel day;
a 22.5 blowout at highway speed is a steering event, so pressure discipline is safety discipline.

## Holding tanks and fresh water | system: plumbing | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Fresh water 95 gallons, grey 60 gallons, black 40 gallons, water heater 10 gallons. Plumbing is
TuffPEX throughout, with low point drain valves, a fresh water gravity fill, and heated water and
holding tank compartments for cold weather. The tank level monitor panel is inside at the
centralized control panel. Dual holding tanks have the San-T-Flush rinse system; run it at dump
time to keep black tank sensors honest. The water pump is a variable speed Aquatec with
multi-point pump switching (switches at more than one location).

## Water heater | system: appliances | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

10 gallon water heater, direct spark ignition, runs on 120V electric or propane (some coaches got
the optional 16 gallon output upgrade). If there is no hot water on propane, listen for the igniter
click cycle at the water heater bay; on electric, confirm shore or generator power and the heater's
120V switch. Both sources can run together for faster recovery.

## Propane system | system: propane | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Appliance propane tank is 38 gallons water capacity; safety code fills to 80%, so usable is about
30 gallons. Propane feeds the cooktop, the fridge on gas mode, the water heater, and the furnace.
There is a propane detector alarm inside. IMPORTANT: any propane smell, hiss, or leak is a
make-safe-and-escalate situation, never a DIY fix. Shut the tank valve, ventilate, get everyone
out, and call the Keeper.

## Generator | system: generator | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Onan 7.5 kW Quiet Diesel generator, drawing from the main 125 gallon diesel tank. Like most
diesel-tank setups it cannot run the tank dry: the generator pickup sits high so it stops drawing
around a quarter tank. If the generator cranks but will not start, first check: main tank above a
quarter, generator breakers (on the set itself) not tripped, and the prime cycle (hold the start
switch in the stop/prime position per the Onan label). Run the generator monthly under load
(an air conditioner is a good load) to keep it healthy. Some coaches have the automatic generator
start option tied to the inverter.

## House and chassis electrical | system: electrical | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

50 amp shore power with an Energy Management System (soft start, sheds loads when the pedestal is
weak). Inverter is a 2000 watt unit (Magnum on most coaches) feeding selected outlets and TVs.
House batteries: four Group 27 AGM maintenance-free 12V. Chassis batteries: two 12V at 1000 cold
cranking amps each. There is a battery disconnect switch, an electronic battery control center, a
battery monitor, and an auxiliary start switch on the dash that bridges house batteries to start
the engine when chassis batteries are low. A solar panel on the front A/C shroud trickles the
batteries. If outlets are dead but lights work, check the inverter's own breakers and its remote
panel before anything else.

## Slides (slide-out rooms) | system: slides | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure + owner forums | type: manual

She has three slide-out rooms on the 40E and 40J floorplans, four on the 40L. Interlocks: the coach
generally wants the parking brake set and the leveling jacks down before slides will move; if a
slide will not budge, check those first, plus house battery voltage. Owner forums report the slide
relay control boards as the usual suspect when a slide clicks or barely moves with good power
(Power Gear boards on many Fleetwoods of this era). Verify her exact slide hardware against the
data plates in the slide bays before ordering parts.

## Leveling jacks | system: chassis | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure + owner forums | type: manual

4-point automatic hydraulic leveling jacks. Normal sequence: park, parking brake, engine running,
auto-level, then slides. Store the jacks fully before driving; a jacks-down alarm means stop.
Forum wisdom for jacks that will not retract: engine on for full voltage, hold the store button,
and check hydraulic fluid level at the pump reservoir (usually in a forward bay). A jack that
drops overnight or a visible hydraulic leak is a Keeper call, not a DIY repair.

## Climate: roof airs, furnace, vents | system: hvac | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Dual high-efficiency roof air conditioners (heat pump option on some coaches), a propane furnace,
and power roof vents with rain sensors in the galley, bath, and shower. Heat pumps stop helping
below about 40F; below that use the furnace. If one roof air quits on shore power, check the EMS
display first; a weak pedestal makes the EMS shed an A/C on purpose.

## Fridge and galley | system: appliances | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Standard fridge is a Norcold 12 cu ft 4-door running 120V or propane with icemaker (some coaches
have the optional Dometic 14 cu ft side-by-side). On gas mode the fridge needs to be roughly level
to cool properly; parked badly off-level for a day can hurt an absorption fridge. Galley has a GE
convection microwave, a recessed 3-burner sealed cooktop, and a solid surface countertop. If the
fridge stops cooling on propane but works on electric, the burner and flue need cleaning, which is
a Keeper task.

## Dimensions for driving and parking | system: general | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Overall height 12 feet 10 inches with roof airs; hold 13 feet as her clearance number and check
every low bridge. Length is 40'11.5" (40E) or 39'11.5" (40J and 40L); width 101 inches plus
mirrors. Interior ceiling is 84 inches. She carries a rear vision camera with one-way audio, four
turn signal lamps per side, and docking lights for night arrivals.

## Safety equipment aboard | system: general | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Carbon monoxide detector, propane detector, smoke detector, fire extinguisher, and a back-up
alarm. Test all three detectors at the start of every voyage; detector age matters, so note
replacement dates in the maintenance log. Any CO alarm is a stop-everything event: engine and
generator off, windows open, everyone out, call the Keeper.

## Known aging points on this coach | system: general | source: owner forums (iRV2, RV Insider) | type: forum_wisdom

Owner-reported patterns on 2006 Revolution LEs worth watching: clearcoat failing on the roof
radius and front and rear caps (cosmetic), air system fittings behind the windshield fairing
working loose (air hiss at the front), generator hard-start when not exercised, and slide relay
boards. None of these are voyage-enders; log symptoms in the maintenance log with photos so the
Keeper can plan the fix.

## Exterior conveniences worth knowing | system: general | source: 2006 Revolution LE brochure | type: manual

Electric patio awning plus window awnings, exterior rinse hose, exterior convenience light at the
dump valves, heated remote-adjust mirrors, keyless entry remote, power entry step with a
last-out feature, Hadley air horns, two-piece rear ladder, and lighted luggage bays with lockable
latches. The dump bay light and rinse hose make night dumps civilized.
