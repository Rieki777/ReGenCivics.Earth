# Crowdpooling: How It All Works

A plain-language guide to the whole model. For campaign stewards, contributors, partners, and anyone deciding whether to run a campaign. This describes the platform as it will work once the current build lands.

---

## The one-paragraph version

Crowdpooling is a barn raising with a public ledger. A land project lists everything it needs to come alive: hours, roles, tools, vehicles, materials, lumber, plants, knowledge, and money. People claim the pieces they can bring. The platform tracks every non-financial contribution from pledge to delivery to thank-you, celebrates the people who show up, and grows their Living Tree in the game. Money flows through trusted partners who already do it well: Ma Earth for donations with matching grants, GoSteward for loans that investors earn a return on. We track the capital money can't buy. Our partners handle the money.

## Why we built it this way

Crowdfunding platforms pool one thing: cash. Real projects need eight forms of capital to get off the ground, and most of what a community can give is time, skill, tools, and materials. Nobody tracks that side well. Meanwhile Ma Earth and GoSteward already run excellent financial rails, so we recommend them instead of duplicating them. The result is a page that shows a project's whole capital stack in one place: what's been given in-kind through us, donated and matched through Ma Earth, and loaned through GoSteward. That view exists nowhere else.

## The pieces

**A campaign** is a land project's public ask. It grows out of the project's incubator application, gets reviewed before going live, runs for a set window (usually 90 days), and stays up afterward as a record of what the community built together.

**A need** is one slot on the campaign. Every need says exactly what, how many, and by when:

- Items: "40 cedar posts, 12 of 40 claimed"
- Roles: "Tool librarian for August, 0 of 1 filled"
- Shifts: "Saturday work party, 9am to 1pm, 8 of 12 spots"
- Tool loans: "Wood chipper for June, we're the custodian, condition checked at handoff and return"
- Knowledge: "2-hour pond design consult"
- Money: a link to the project's Ma Earth or GoSteward page

Big needs can be filled by many people together. A 40-hour need fills with ten 4-hour pledges.

**A claim** is a person saying "I've got this one." Claims move through a simple life:

1. **Pledged.** You claim a need. No account required, just your name and email.
2. **Accepted.** The steward confirms you're a fit. Your name goes on the slot. You'll get reminders so nothing slips.
3. **Delivered.** The thing actually arrived: the shift happened, the lumber showed up, the consult was given. This is the moment that counts. Everything of value happens on delivery, never on promises.
4. **Thanked.** The steward closes the loop with a note or photo showing your contribution in use. You get it directly.

Claims that don't land simply expire and the slot reopens for someone else. Time and resources count only when delivered.

## What contributors get

No platform tokens. You're pooling your resources into a project, and the recognition works like this:

- **Your Living Tree grows.** Every delivered contribution is recorded in your player profile under its form of capital (material, living, intellectual, and so on) and feeds the tree on your profile.
- **Contribution score.** Delivered contributions earn game score, which moves you through the citizenship tiers.
- **Gratitude.** Anyone in the community can send gratitude on your contribution, and gratitude carries real weight in the game's economy.
- **Project tokens from Hypha.** Projects can formalize significant contributions through their Hypha DHO, which issues the project's own tokens to contributors. That's between you and the project, on their terms, through the bridge we've already built.
- **The story.** Your name on the slot, your moment in the pool ledger, the thank-you photo of your lumber in the wall.

## What campaign stewards need to know

You are the named human at the center of the campaign. The research on every platform like this is blunt: campaigns with an active steward thrive and campaigns without one die. Here's your job and your toolkit:

**Before launch**
- Your campaign builds from your incubator application, so most fields prefill.
- List specific needs, not vague asks. "Volunteers wanted" dies. "3 of 5 carpenters found" recruits.
- Spread the sizes: a third small asks (an hour, a hand tool), a third medium, a third large. Everyone should find an entry point.
- Line up your first claims before going public. Pools that start at zero stay at zero; pools that open a quarter full keep filling.
- If you want donations, open a Ma Earth page (they match donations with grants, and many small donors multiply your match). If you need a loan, apply with GoSteward (they lend to established regenerative businesses and help architect your whole capital stack). Link both on your campaign.

**While live**
- Review claims quickly. Accept the fits, release the rest.
- Mark things delivered when they arrive. Delivery is what moves your progress bars and what earns your contributors their recognition.
- Thank every delivered contribution with a note or photo. This is required, and it's the single strongest driver of people coming back.
- Post updates. Write them like letters from the land, short and real. Campaigns that post updates get sustained contributions; silent pages read as dead.
- Expect the mid-campaign lull. It happens to everyone. Plan something for the middle: a work party, a stretch need, a milestone celebration.
- You'll get a weekly digest of unfilled needs, expiring claims, and new followers so nothing falls through.

**After**
- The page becomes your project's permanent record: the ledger, the gratitude wall, the story.
- A funded campaign moves your project toward Established status in the game.

## How money works (and why we never touch it)

We don't process payments, hold funds, or take a cut. Financial needs on a campaign are links to partners:

- **Ma Earth** (maearth.com) takes donations and matches them with grants from a shared pool. Their matching rewards many small donors over a few big ones, which means the community you gather here directly multiplies the money there. Funding runs in rounds.
- **GoSteward** (gosteward.com) arranges loans for established regenerative businesses. Investors fund the loan and earn a return. They also help projects design their full capital stack: the right mix of loans, grants, equity, and community support. Apply for a loan with them directly; we recommend, they decide.

Your campaign page shows live totals from both, read directly from their pages, so the whole stack is visible in one place.

## How it connects to the rest of the ecosystem

- **The incubator**: campaigns grow out of accepted incubator applications. Funded campaigns advance the project's standing.
- **The game**: delivered contributions feed contribution score, capital scores, the Living Tree, and citizenship tiers. Gratitude flows on contributions the same way it flows everywhere else.
- **Hypha**: on-chain formalization goes through the bridge we've already built. Projects issue their own tokens to contributors through their DHO.
- **The forum**: campaigns link to community discussion threads.
- **Seasons**: crowdpooling runs in seasons alongside the rest of the game. Our first season of live campaigns opens late 2026 / early 2027. Everything on the site before then is clearly labeled as an example.

## What we're building right now

So you know what's changing under the hood, in order:

1. **One honest system.** The demo gallery and the real campaign engine merge. Real campaigns become browsable at last, share links work per campaign, contributor counts are real, notification emails turn back on, and every example is labeled as an example.
2. **The needs registry.** Needs with exact quantities and deadlines, the claim lifecycle above, reminders, expiring claims that reopen automatically, and progress bars that show delivered work solid and promised work as a light overlay.
3. **Alive and grateful.** The public pool ledger, campaign updates, the required thank-you step, gratitude on contributions, follow buttons, weekly steward digests, and share links that show you what your share brought in.
4. **Partners and the capital stack.** Ma Earth and GoSteward blocks on every campaign, live financial totals, the full capital stack visual, and a short quiz that routes projects to the right partner.
5. **Hypha formalization.** One-click path from a delivered contribution to a project's DHO for project token issuance.
6. **Polish.** One unified visual language across every crowdpooling surface, a map of projects, and an accessibility pass so this works for everyone, tech-comfortable or not.

## Principles we won't break

- Nothing of value moves on a promise. Delivery is the moment that counts.
- Real numbers or no numbers. No fake avatars, no padded counts, examples always labeled.
- Names on contributions by default, anonymity always available.
- We celebrate people, we never rank them. No leaderboards.
- Money belongs with partners who do it well.
- The measurable capitals get measured. The rest (social, cultural, spiritual) get honored through story and gratitude, because a spreadsheet can't hold them.
